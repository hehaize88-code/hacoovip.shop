QC

How to read QC photos before shipping

What to inspect on clothing, shoes and accessories before consolidating.

Updated August 2026Reading time: 10 min

QC photographs are most useful when you approach them with a checklist rather than a vague feeling. USFans says purchased items are checked at its warehouse, and many current product pages advertise 3–7 free HD quality-inspection photographs. Those images can reveal visible mistakes before international shipping, but only if you know what each angle can and cannot prove. Build the habit before your first order: preserve the seller reference, decide which differences would justify a return, and inspect promptly after warehouse arrival. That turns QC from passive browsing into a decision tool with a clear purpose and deadline.

01

What warehouse QC can genuinely tell you

A warehouse photograph can confirm the item that arrived, the visible colour, approximate silhouette, quantity, labelled size and obvious damage. It can also reveal uneven print placement, missing hardware, major stains, poor alignment or a product that is plainly different from the ordered variant. That is already valuable: correcting a visible error while the item is in the warehouse is normally more practical than discovering it after an international parcel reaches you.

QC cannot guarantee authenticity, long-term durability, fabric composition, internal construction or a perfect fit. Lighting changes colour; camera distance affects proportions; a folded garment can hide its shape. Treat every image as evidence with limits. The goal is not to convince yourself that the item is flawless. The goal is to identify differences that are visible, material to your decision and still actionable before parcel submission.

02

Prepare a reference before the item arrives

Good inspection starts at purchase. Save the seller’s main image, the exact selected variant, the size chart and any detail that influenced your choice. If you care about a particular embroidery position, sole colour or pocket design, record it. Without a reference, QC becomes a memory test, and similar products quickly blur together. A small order note makes it possible to compare what was promised with what physically reached the warehouse.

Use objective criteria whenever possible. Replace “I want it oversized” with a target chest width and length. Replace “the logo should look right” with its expected position relative to a seam or pocket. Not every seller provides reliable measurements, but recording what is available exposes gaps before ordering. It also gives you a precise request if an extra warehouse measurement is needed.

03

Inspect clothing in a fixed sequence

For tops and jackets, begin with the full front and back. Check overall shape, symmetry, colour panels, print or embroidery placement, zipper direction and the number of pockets. Move to the neck and size label, then inspect cuffs, hem, major seams and visible hardware. For trousers, compare waist style, rise, leg shape, pocket placement and hem. If measurements are shown, compare them with the listing’s measurement method rather than assuming two sellers measure in the same way.

Fabric weight and hand-feel are difficult to judge from a photograph, so avoid unsupported conclusions. You can sometimes see thinness against strong light or obvious surface texture, but a camera does not establish material quality. Focus first on discrepancies that can be demonstrated: wrong colour, wrong label, missing feature, visible hole, stain, crooked graphic or measurement outside an acceptable tolerance.

04

Inspect footwear as a pair, not two objects

Compare the left and right shoe together from the front, sides, heel and top. Look at toe shape, panel alignment, lace placement, sole colour, heel height, stitching paths and symmetry. A tilted camera can create false differences, so use more than one angle before judging. Check the size label against the ordered size and, when fit risk is high, consider whether an insole or outsole measurement is available.

Boxes add volume and can be damaged during domestic delivery without the shoes themselves being affected. Decide whether the box is important before parcel packing. Removing it may reduce volumetric weight, while keeping it may offer structure or collector value. There is no universal right answer; connect the choice to route pricing, protection and your reason for buying.

05

Bags, accessories and small parts

For bags, check the overall shape, handle symmetry, strap inclusion, closures, corners, lining where visible and the placement of external details. Small accessories require a quantity check: pairs, screws, charms, spare laces or detachable pieces are easy to overlook. Reflective or metallic surfaces can look different under warehouse lights, so compare several images before calling a colour or finish defective.

Electronics, liquids, batteries, magnets and other restricted items also have a logistics dimension. A product can look correct yet reduce the routes available for the entire parcel. Check restrictions before acceptance and consolidation. The platform’s own product notices acknowledge that prohibited or limited goods, line limitations, weight and volume can affect shipping options.

06

Request more evidence only when it changes a decision

USFans listings commonly advertise 3–7 free HD QC images, which may be sufficient for a straightforward product. Ask for more only when a decision-critical detail is missing: a measurement, serial area, damaged corner, print alignment or the contents of a set. Before requesting a paid service, confirm the current charge and describe the desired angle precisely. “More photos” can produce another unhelpful view; “photograph the inside size label straight-on” is much clearer.

Do not mistake image quantity for inspection quality. Five planned views can be more useful than fifteen random ones. Keep your request short, polite and tied to an action. If the new evidence shows the item meets your tolerance, accept it. If it confirms a material mismatch, begin the applicable after-sales process rather than repeatedly requesting photos that will not change the outcome.

07

Use the five-day return statement carefully

Many current product pages state that a return application can be made within five days after warehouse arrival and that agents will negotiate with the seller. Check the terms attached to your actual order immediately because seller acceptance, customisation, hygiene rules, domestic return freight and item condition may matter. The statement is a reason to inspect promptly, not a guarantee that every return is free or approved.

A strong issue report names the ordered variant, identifies the visible mismatch, points to the relevant QC image and states the requested resolution. Keep screenshots of the listing and the order selection. This evidence makes the case understandable without emotional language. Do not submit the item in an international parcel while the case is open.

08

The final ten-point QC check

Before acceptance, confirm: item identity, variant, labelled size, quantity, front view, back view, left-right symmetry, visible damage, included parts and any measurement that controls fit. Then ask one final question: is the remaining uncertainty something another photograph can resolve? If yes, request the specific evidence. If not, decide whether the uncertainty is acceptable or whether the item should be returned under the current terms.

QC works best as a disciplined pause between purchase and shipping. It is neither a guarantee nor a decorative gallery. Used properly, it turns warehouse time into a practical quality-control window and gives you a chance to keep obvious problems out of an expensive international parcel.

What matters, without the noise.

Judge visible facts, compare against a saved reference, request only decision-changing evidence, and act before the applicable after-sales window closes. Review every stored item as soon as its photographs appear, because a disciplined early decision preserves more practical options than a rushed inspection during parcel submission.
