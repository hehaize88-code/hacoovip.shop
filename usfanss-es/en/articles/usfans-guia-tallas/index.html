Sizing

USFans size guide: measure clothing and shoes before ordering

Compare centimetres, the seller’s current size chart and warehouse QC instead of relying on a generic size conversion.

October 7, 2026Reading time: 7 min

Choosing a size through USFans means checking the seller’s listing and comparing measurements. The agent’s name does not define a universal sizing system. An M, XL or European shoe number can describe different cuts and shapes in different listings. This method connects an item that already fits you with the current size chart and the evidence you can request after warehouse arrival. It reduces uncertainty without claiming that a photograph can guarantee comfort.

01

Start with a reference you actually wear

Choose a reference from the same category with the fit you want. For a thick sweatshirt, use another sweatshirt rather than a stretchy T-shirt. Lay it on a flat surface and record how each measurement is taken. Chest width from armpit to armpit, length from a defined starting point, shoulders and sleeves usually provide more context than the letter on the label. Keep a photograph of your measuring method.

Distinguish garment measurements from body measurements. A seller’s chart may give a full circumference while you have measured a flat width. Unless both figures use the same method, they are not directly comparable. Doubling a flat measurement does not automatically produce a recommended body size either: the cut, material and intended ease still matter.

Write down which dimensions are essential and which differences you could tolerate. This gives you a decision rule before you look at available sizes. It also prevents a tempting photograph or a low price from making you overlook the dimension most likely to make the item unsuitable.

02

Read the chart for the current variant

Open the exact listing you intend to order and find the chart associated with that version. Check units, column labels and any measurement tolerance the seller states. A chart from another colour, production version or older listing may not apply. Save the image or values you relied on alongside the URL and exact variant.

If a decisive dimension is missing, ask before ordering. A reply such as “normal sizing” does not establish a jacket’s chest width or a pair of trousers’ length. Specify the starting point, finishing point and unit. If the seller cannot provide enough information, consider a better-documented listing. Choosing a size at random simply moves the uncertainty to the warehouse stage.

03

Worked example: compare two sweatshirt sizes

Your reference sweatshirt measures 58 cm across the chest and 70 cm in length when laid flat. A fictional chart lists M as 57 cm by 69 cm and L as 60 cm by 72 cm. The useful comparison is that M is 1 cm smaller on both dimensions, while L is 2 cm larger. These are illustrative numbers, not a real seller’s chart or a universal size recommendation.

Next, decide which difference fits your intended result. Someone seeking the same fit may choose differently from someone wanting a roomier silhouette. Check sleeves and shoulders too, because a similar chest measurement does not guarantee similar proportions. If the listing states a tolerance, compare it with the range you could accept before making a selection.

Do not resolve a decisive mismatch by averaging the sizes mentally. If neither option satisfies the measurements that matter, the cut may not suit your needs. Record why you chose a size and what you will check at arrival. That prior decision rule makes it easier to judge QC evidence without accepting an unsuitable item merely because you have waited for it.

04

Trousers, jackets and stretch fabrics need clear methods

For trousers, check the waist using the seller’s method, together with rise, thigh width and length. An elastic waist measured at rest is not the same as its maximum stretched circumference. Request clarification about the fabric’s state during measurement instead of comparing it with an unrelated body chart. Your reference should be a garment used in a similar way, not a size you remember buying elsewhere.

For jackets and clothing worn over other layers, consider the space and movement you want. Sleeve length may be measured from the shoulder or another point; a dropped shoulder changes how the figures relate to fit. Do not add an arbitrary number of centimetres to every measurement. Define the intended use first, then compare equivalent dimensions.

05

Shoes: understand centimetres and shape before converting sizes

For footwear, measure both feet consistently and consider the longer measurement when reading the model’s chart. Establish what a centimetre column represents: recommended foot length, insole length or something else. Those figures are not interchangeable. Do not move EU, US or UK conversions between sellers without checking the actual model and chart.

Compare with a pair you wear for a similar activity and consider the socks you intend to use. Width, toe shape and internal volume can matter even when length appears suitable. An outsole measurement does not reveal internal space. If you request an insole photograph, use it as supplementary evidence and make sure the measurement method is clear.

No single photograph establishes how a shoe will feel while walking. Keep that limitation separate from visible checks such as matching labels, the ordered size and obvious differences between the two shoes. If the available information leaves a significant fit risk, decide whether that uncertainty is acceptable before arranging international shipping.

06

Ask for a QC measurement that can answer your question

After warehouse arrival, first confirm product identity, variant and label. If the concern is size, request a view with the garment flat, the measuring tape visible and both endpoints identifiable. For example, ask for flat chest width from armpit to armpit without stretching, with the zero mark and final reading visible. Check whether the request is available and what it costs before selecting it.

Avoid simply asking staff to check whether the size is right. They do not know your body or preferred fit. Also avoid estimating centimetres from an angled photograph without a scale. If one endpoint cannot be read, request clarification about that particular image. A value inferred from pixels should not be presented as a physical measurement of the garment.

07

Resolve differences before consolidation

Compare the warehouse measurement with the saved chart and your reference, allowing for the stated method and tolerance. If there is a discrepancy, retain the photo, ordered option and expected value. Ask whether it reflects an incorrect variant, a different measurement method or the actual dimensions of the product. Those are different situations and may require different responses.

Check the conditions and deadlines for your order promptly. Whether an exchange or return is possible depends on that transaction; this guide does not guarantee approval. Keep the item out of an international parcel while the decision remains open. If you accept a difference, record precisely what you accepted so that the decision is not lost among several similar purchases.

08

Keep useful records after delivery

After receiving the item, add notes about the actual fit, measurements and use. A successful size from one listing is a reference for that product, not for the whole platform. Recheck if the listing changes. Separate personal preferences from observable dimensions so that you remember why the garment worked for you.

A useful record includes the URL, product reference, variant, size, chart, QC measurement, date and fit notes. A small set of consistent references is more useful than many recommendations without context. Complete the sizing decision with the full budget: replacing or returning an item can involve additional time and costs.
