Finding QC photos

How to Find Kakobuy QC Photos From a Weidian or Taobao Link

Look up warehouse photos by the original item link, match the size and colour, and decide what to do when a KakoBuy QC search returns no pictures.

By Kakobuys.pro Research Desk7 min read1284 words3 November 2026

If you already placed an order, start with the warehouse entry for that item in your KakoBuy account. If you are still choosing what to buy, start with the original Weidian or Taobao link. These two searches answer different questions. One can show what arrived for your order. The other can turn up pictures contributed from earlier orders of the same listing. A useful result tells you which item the camera faced and which question the picture can answer.

Which pictures are you looking for?

Where the photo appearsWhat you can use it forWhat remains open
Seller’s listingIdentify the advertised design and available options.What the seller will send for your order.
Public QC albumSee how an earlier order connected to this listing looked at a warehouse.Whether the colour, size, version or current stock matches.
Your warehouse orderCompare the received piece with the options in your receipt.Anything outside the frame, fit on your body and performance in use.

A ruler or a green background can appear in any of these pictures. To identify the source, look at the page containing the image. A gallery attached to a particular warehouse order gives you a stronger connection to that purchase than a standalone image shared without its caption. Keep the page link alongside any detail you record.

1. Recover the original item link

Open the actual product from your spreadsheet. A category, a seller profile and a screenshot usually do not identify one listing. In a standard Weidian item URL, the number after itemID= identifies the listing. A standard Taobao item URL often contains item.htm?id= followed by the item number. Record the marketplace as well as the number. Two strings of digits from different marketplaces do not establish a match.

On a phone, use the product’s share or copy-link option. If it gives you a shortened address, open it normally and confirm that you reach the same product and seller. With an agent link, look for a displayed source link or a marketplace address inside the link. Some tools accept the entire agent URL. If the destination only opens a shop, ask for the individual product link. Recording the shop name and visible title can help you spot a misdirected link later.

2. Try a public lookup and verify what it found

Paste the original product address into a public lookup that supports its marketplace. For example, the independent QC Finder documents its accepted links and explains that its albums can come from several agents. A result reached through a KakoBuy link can therefore show a different warehouse. Check the source and item number on the result before you start looking at the shoes or clothes.

Take the actual link example on that finder. Its KakoBuy address contains an encoded Weidian address. The underlying product identifier is 7659337676. Copy that number from the example and compare it with the number after itemID= in the source address. This checks that both paths refer to the same listing. It does not establish that a public album exists today, that a particular size was photographed or that anything is currently in stock. Write down the date you performed the lookup if you intend to return to it.

3. Match the variant before examining the finish

A listing may sell several colours, versions and sizes behind one item number. Select the combination you are considering and save the exact option wording. On the album page, look for the options recorded for that earlier order. Read any visible label and compare the overall shape, colour panels and included pieces. Use both the text and the photograph. Thumbnails sometimes stay the same when a dropdown selection changes.

Suppose you want the grey pair in EU 42 and a result shows a grey pair labelled EU 40. Use it to see where the upper meets the sole or where the seller places a patch. It cannot settle the insole length for the 42. A second album showing EU 42 in black can help you read its size label, but still leaves the grey material and your individual pair unanswered. Keep those observations separate even if the two albums have the same seller and date.

4. Compare recent and older albums without mixing orders

If several records match the listing, start with the closest variant and the most relevant date. Open a single album and inspect its photos together. Record the colour, size, any version wording, date if shown and which details are actually visible. A recent full-pair image and an older close-up might both be useful, but using the close-up to judge a seam on the newer pair quietly combines evidence from different units.

Look for an angle that could settle your buying question. A straight rear view can show whether two heel counters look aligned. A label close-up can help identify a marked size. For a bag, a front image may show the proportions while leaving the inside compartments entirely hidden. If a result lacks a date, leave the date field blank instead of assigning the upload day of the post that linked to it.

5. When the finder shows no photos

First confirm that the address opens the intended product, the lookup covers that marketplace and the item number survived the redirect. If you started with a shared agent link, retry the original marketplace link once. Search services have different coverage. An empty response tells you what that particular lookup returned at that moment. It cannot tell you that the item has never been ordered or that it passed an inspection.

Next decide what information you need before spending money. For a hoodie, that might be the chest width of a selected size. Ask the seller for the size chart and measuring method, or postpone the decision if a photograph of the actual item is essential. If you already own the order, look for your item’s warehouse entry. KakoBuy describes the arrival and inspection sequence in its help page on warehouse arrival. A carrier delivery notification and a completed warehouse inspection are separate stages.

6. Request one photograph that can change your decision

Once the warehouse record is available, compare it with the saved order options. If an important point is still hidden, use the service or support option available for that order and check any stated fee before confirming. Be precise about the piece, angle and unit. For example, “Please lay the left removable insole flat beside a centimetre ruler, with the heel and toe both visible.” An outsole measurement answers a different question. If the insole cannot be removed, ask which measurement can be taken and compare like with like.

For a two-piece set, ask to see both pieces together and their individual labels. For a damaged zip, identify the side and request a close view showing the pull and adjoining teeth. If a mismatch remains, keep the images and current order details together and check the available after-sales action before submitting the parcel. For a systematic review once the correct album is open, follow the seven-frame QC photo checklist.

A short record to keep with your shortlist

  • Original marketplace, product URL, item number and seller shown when checked.
  • Exact version, colour, marked size, quantity and any included accessories.
  • Image source, album date when available, and whether the order belongs to you.
  • One visible detail, one unanswered question and the action that would resolve it.

This record stays useful when a seller changes a cover photo or an album disappears. You can revisit the listing, reconstruct the choice you made and explain exactly which extra view you need, without asking someone to judge an entire product from a single picture.
