Apparel QC

Kakobuy Hoodie and Streetwear QC: Measurements, Prints and Construction

A category-specific QC method for Kakobuy hoodies, sweatshirts and streetwear: verify the source, measure the garment and inspect panels, ribbing and artwork.

By Kakobuys.pro Research Desk12 min read1,362 wordsReviewed July 30, 2026

Hoodies and sweatshirts look simple in a product card, but they contain several fit and construction decisions that a front photo can hide. Shoulder width changes the silhouette. Body length changes layering. Ribbing can pull unevenly. A print can be centred relative to the image crop but not the actual garment. The QC process should make those fields visible.

A Kakobuy hoodie spreadsheet helps with discovery when it records a live destination and representative product image. It becomes misleading when every category uses a generic stock photo or when “oversized” replaces measurements. Use the row to reach a current product record, then use the warehouse item to decide.

Apparel review orderOrdered style and size → garment laid flat → chest and length measurements → shoulder and side-seam balance → hood, cuffs and hem → print or embroidery placement → visible defects and deadline.

Verify the destination and selected garment

Open the source listing and compare it with the Kakobuy order. Record the seller, exact style, colour, size, fabric claim, price and size chart shown on the order date. A seller can group crewnecks, pullover hoodies, zip hoodies and several fabric weights under one page. The cover image may not represent the selected option.

At warehouse arrival, confirm whether the garment has the expected hood, zip, pocket layout, colour and artwork. Check the sewn size label, but do not stop there. A correct label can be attached to a garment whose measurements differ from the seller chart.

Lay the garment flat before measuring

Ask for chest width from underarm seam to underarm seam, back length from the relevant collar point to hem, and sleeve length using the seller’s stated method. For drop-shoulder garments, a neck-to-cuff measurement may be more reproducible than a conventional shoulder seam. Record whether the fabric is relaxed or stretched.

Compare the warehouse numbers with a similar garment you own, measured the same way. Doubling a flat chest width approximates circumference but does not account for fabric thickness or stretch. “Oversized” is a style description, not a number.

Check shoulder, body and side-seam geometry

Use a straight overhead image. Compare the left and right shoulder slope, sleeve opening and body side. Side seams should not visibly spiral when the garment is laid naturally. The hem should sit at a consistent level unless the design intentionally uses a drop tail or asymmetric cut.

Camera angle can create a false imbalance. Use the warehouse mat or table edge as a reference and request a centred repeat when one sleeve appears much longer only in one photo. A persistent difference across front and rear views is stronger evidence.

Inspect the hood, neckline and drawstring exits

For hoodies, check that the hood seam is centred and that both sides attach evenly at the neckline. Drawstring eyelets should sit at comparable heights, and the cords should pass through the expected channel. Unequal cord ends can be adjusted; visibly offset eyelets or a twisted hood panel cannot.

For crewnecks, inspect collar ribbing for a smooth oval and even attachment. A wavy collar can result from stretching during sewing. Photos cannot tell you how the rib will recover after washing, but they can reveal obvious twisting or puckering before shipment.

Close zips and align pockets

On a zip hoodie, request a closed front view. Follow the zipper from neck to hem and check whether artwork, seams and ribbing meet at the same height. A zipper that pulls one panel upward may indicate unequal front pieces or a misaligned installation. A photo cannot fully test repeated zip function, but it can show track damage and visible skew.

Compare kangaroo-pocket corners or separate pocket heights with the centre line and hem. Pocket openings should be reasonably symmetrical. Ask for a close-up only when a defect could change the decision; more random images are not automatically better.

Use garment landmarks to judge artwork

Check prints and embroidery relative to the neckline, centre front, side seams and pocket. A design can look centred inside a cropped photo while sitting off-centre on the garment. For back prints, use the collar and side seams as reference points. Compare sleeve artwork with cuff and shoulder positions.

Inspect visible edges for lifting, cracks, bleeding, missing fill and loose embroidery threads. Lighting can alter colour and make raised ink look uneven. Request a neutral close-up when the effect appears only under flash. Photographs do not establish wash durability or fibre content.

Check cuffs, waistband and fabric surface

Compare both cuff heights and openings. Ribbing should attach evenly and the waistband should not pull one side upward. Look for holes, stains, snags, severe pilling or inconsistent dye. Fold shadows should not be labelled defects without another angle.

Fabric weight claims require trustworthy measurement or documentation; a photograph cannot prove grams per square metre. A dense-looking brushed interior may still differ from the listing. State the limit instead of converting appearance into a material guarantee.

Separate visible QC from authenticity claims

A warehouse photo can show the location of a label, mark or decorative element. It cannot prove authenticity, licensing or lawful import. Keep those questions outside a visual quality score. The practical visible checks are identity, size, alignment, construction and damage.

This distinction also improves spreadsheet language. “QC checked” should mean named visible fields were reviewed on a date. It should not mean every future order from a seller will have identical fabric or construction.

Return or accept using measurable differences

If the garment differs from the order, combine the source chart, selected option and warehouse measurement. “Ordered size chart chest 60 cm; warehouse tape shows 55 cm” is actionable. For artwork, mark the centre line or reference seam rather than saying it “looks strange.”

Kakobuy’s publicly indexed help describes a five-day return/exchange guarantee for eligible warehouse orders, subject to seller and product conditions. Check the current state and deadline. A garment sent for custom print or altered by request may have different options.

Pack soft apparel without creating new problems

Hoodies can often be packed more compactly than rigid goods, but strong compression may crease thick prints, structured embroidery or decorative hardware. Group soft clothing thoughtfully and keep sharp accessories away from printed surfaces. If retail packaging provides little protection, removal may reduce volume; confirm the current service and route rules.

Compare actual and dimensional parcel scenarios after consolidation. A puffy fleece can create volume even when its scale weight is modest. Do not promise a fixed freight saving from compression because the route may round to the same chargeable step.

Twelve-point streetwear checklist

  1. Live source and seller still match the saved row.
  2. Garment type, colour, size and artwork match the order.
  3. Chest width and body length are measured flat.
  4. Sleeve measurement uses a defined starting point.
  5. Shoulders and side seams are compared in a centred view.
  6. Hood or collar sits centrally.
  7. Zip closes straight when applicable.
  8. Pockets sit at comparable heights.
  9. Print or embroidery uses garment landmarks.
  10. Cuffs and waistband are compared side by side.
  11. Visible stains, snags and holes are checked in neutral light.
  12. Packing protects artwork without unnecessary volume.

A strong hoodie spreadsheet does not need thousands of vague rows. It needs current product routes, actual representative images, clear names and category-specific questions. Measurements and warehouse evidence do the work that marketing labels cannot.
