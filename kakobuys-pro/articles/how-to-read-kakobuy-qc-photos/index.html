File A1 / photo reading

How to Read Kakobuy QC Photos: A Seven-Frame Checklist

A 1,200+ word method for matching an order, reading shape and measurements, handling missing images and recording visible defects without overclaiming.

Kakobuys.pro Field Desk15 min reading timeSource check 30 July 2026

QC review should not begin with the question “does it look good?” That question is too broad to produce a reliable decision. A stronger review asks a sequence of smaller questions: is this the ordered variation, does the overall geometry make sense, do the measurements support the selected size, and is any visible defect important enough to stop the parcel?

This file uses a seven-frame reading order. It does not assume that every Kakobuy order contains exactly seven photographs. “Frame” means a distinct evidence task; several tasks may be answered by one photograph, while a missing task may require an additional view.

Field-desk ruleIdentity comes before quality. Whole-object geometry comes before tiny details. A missing view stays unresolved. A photograph is never promoted into proof of authenticity, material composition or long-term performance.

Frame 01 — preserve the ordered variation

Before payment, save the source URL, option name, colour, marked size, quantity and any seller note that changes what should arrive. Product pages can be edited and spreadsheet labels can become stale. Your own order record is the comparison point that survives those changes.

At warehouse arrival, compare that record with the item entry. A beautifully photographed wrong colour or version is still a failed match. This first pass should also catch duplicated quantities, missing pieces and packaging labels that belong to another order.

Frame 02 — read the complete silhouette

Use the widest, straightest view and temporarily ignore logos. With shoes, compare toe shape, heel height, opening shape and the line where upper meets sole. With clothing, compare shoulder position, body length and sleeve balance. With bags, compare top line, side panels and handle placement.

Perspective can create false asymmetry. Use the table edge, background grid or known straight object as a reference, and check whether the same concern appears from another angle before treating it as a defect.

A warehouse image is also a technical image with its own limits. Wide-angle distortion can enlarge the part closest to the lens, overhead light can wash out pale fabric, and compression can hide fine texture. Before calling something crooked, compare it with a second view taken from a different position. Before calling a colour wrong, compare the photograph with the saved option and allow for lighting rather than relying on a promotional image alone.

The background can help. A straight table edge is useful for judging whether a sole or zipper line really drifts. A ruler is useful only when both ends of the measured section are visible and the product is not folded underneath. If glare, a hand or packaging covers the exact area that matters, the honest result is “not visible,” not “passed.”

Frame 03 — make the measurement answer a real question

A size tag repeats what was selected; it does not prove the finished dimensions. Request or use measurements that map to the buying decision: chest width and length for a top, waist and inseam for trousers, insole or outsole length for footwear, and actual width or strap drop for a bag.

Compare like with like. If the seller measures a garment flat from pit to pit, compare it with a garment you own measured in the same way. Mixing body circumference, garment circumference and half-width produces false conclusions.

Frames 04 and 05 — trace construction before surface

Follow long structural lines: seams, sole joins, zipper tracks, panel borders, piping and repeated hardware. A sudden change in spacing or a line that visibly drifts is usually more informative than one isolated thread.

Then inspect surface issues such as stains, scratches, glue marks, dents and print damage. Light reflection can imitate a scratch and compressed fabric can change after unpacking, so look for the same mark from another angle when the decision is close.

Frame 06 — check labels without overclaiming

Labels can help confirm the ordered size, colour code or model identifier. They can also expose an obvious spelling or alignment issue when that visible detail matters to the buyer.

A label or familiar logo is not independent authenticity evidence. Kakobuys.pro records what can be seen; it does not convert a photographed mark into a verification service.

Shoes need a pair comparison: left and right toe shape, heel height, outsole length and the join between upper and sole. Tops need flat measurements, shoulder balance, collar shape and print placement. Trousers need waist, rise and inseam evidence. Bags need overall dimensions, handle anchoring, edge finishing and a complete view of the hardware set. One generic “stitching looks good” sentence is not a serious QC method for all four categories.

Electronics require a stricter boundary. Kakobuy’s indexed help says its standard inspection of household appliances, digital products and other electronics does not include checking authenticity, function or quality. Photographs may still confirm the model marking, included accessories, plug shape or visible damage, but they cannot establish that a device powers on safely or performs as advertised.

Frame 07 — decide what remains unresolved

End the review with three columns: accepted visible points, visible problems and unanswered questions. This is more useful than a single green-light or red-light label because it shows exactly what the photographs supported.

If a material concern depends on an absent angle or measurement, request that evidence promptly. Kakobuy’s publicly indexed help describes a five-day return/exchange guarantee for eligible items from participating third-party sellers, so a long warehouse-storage allowance should not be mistaken for a long decision window.

On 10 May 2026, Kakobuy published a notice about abnormal, incorrect or unavailable QC images. The notice said the issue affected the display of some QC images and did not affect warehouse storage or normal shipping submission. That distinction is useful: a broken thumbnail does not by itself prove that the physical item is lost, damaged or absent.

Do not guess in either direction. Check the order record, refresh the image set, preserve a screenshot of the problem and use the current support channel if the image remains unavailable or belongs to the wrong item. Shipping without resolving an important missing view turns a temporary information problem into a decision made without evidence.

A useful QC conclusion has one of three forms. “Keep” means the ordered identity matches and the visible concerns are acceptable to this buyer. “Request evidence” means the decision depends on a missing angle, measurement or clarification. “Open after-sales” means a visible mismatch or defect is important enough to use the live order process while the item is still eligible. A numerical score hides these different next steps.

Write the concern in observable language: “the measured chest width appears to be 56 cm while the saved listing shows 60 cm,” or “the right shoe’s heel panel sits higher in both rear views.” Avoid claims about hidden materials, authenticity or future durability. Specific observations are easier to review, easier to attach to the correct image and less likely to become an argument built on a vague feeling.

  1. Confirm ordered variation and quantity.
  2. Read the whole silhouette before details.
  3. Use decision-relevant measurements.
  4. Trace joins, seams and repeated elements.
  5. Check surface defects from more than one angle.
  6. Use labels for identity, not authenticity proof.
  7. Write down what remains unanswered.
