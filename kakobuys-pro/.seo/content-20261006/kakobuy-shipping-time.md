# Kakobuy Shipping Time: From Seller Dispatch to Delivery

Kakobuy shipping time starts with a choice about which clock you are measuring. A seller sending an item to the warehouse is one journey. Warehouse intake, photo review and parcel preparation are separate steps. International transport begins later, and the final delivery scan belongs to another stage again. Adding an advertised international transit estimate to your purchase date can therefore create an unrealistic arrival expectation.

The practical approach is to record the current stage, its starting event and the next event that should appear. This guide explains how to build that record, plan backwards from a deadline and describe a delay accurately. It does not promise a universal delivery time or assume that a particular shipping line serves every destination. The available route and current order information must support each estimate.

## Start with the correct event

A purchase payment, a seller dispatch notice and an international parcel payment are different events. The first establishes that money was paid for an order. It does not establish that the seller handed over a package. A warehouse arrival scan establishes arrival at the facility, but it does not necessarily mean the item is ready for your inspection or has been packed for export.

Write dates next to named events instead of keeping one number called shipping days. Useful entries include purchase payment, seller handover, warehouse arrival, QC availability, parcel submission, parcel payment, carrier acceptance and delivery. Leave a field blank when it has not happened. Replacing an unknown date with an assumed date makes every later calculation look more certain than it is.

| Stage | Evidence to record | Next event to look for |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Seller fulfilment | Correct order and variation, dispatch information | Domestic carrier acceptance |
| Domestic transport | Seller shipment reference and latest scan | Warehouse receipt |
| Warehouse processing | Item status, photographs and unresolved requests | Item ready for parcel selection |
| Parcel preparation | Submitted items, packaging and payment status | International carrier acceptance |
| International delivery | Route, carrier and latest dated event | Destination processing and delivery |

## Seller fulfilment comes before domestic transit

An item marked purchased may still be awaiting seller dispatch. A seller may need to confirm the selected option, prepare stock or resolve an unavailable variation. Do not describe all of that time as slow international shipping. Check whether a domestic tracking reference exists and whether the carrier has actually accepted the shipment, rather than relying only on a label-created event.

For a multi-item order, track each purchase separately. One late item can delay a combined parcel if you decide to wait for everything. That is a consolidation choice, not evidence that the items already in storage are moving slowly. If one product is essential for an event, compare the implications of waiting, shipping available items separately or choosing a different purchase plan before the deadline becomes urgent.

## Warehouse arrival and QC readiness are separate

The warehouse needs to connect an arrival with the relevant order record. Your decision then depends on whether the available photographs and measurements answer the important questions. A missing size measurement can hold up your own approval even when the item has arrived. Record whether you are waiting for processing, additional information, a seller response or your own decision.

Use the [QC photo checklist](/articles/how-to-read-kakobuy-qc-photos/) to make that review focused. Ask for a specific view, measurement or clarification when it matters. A request saying that the sleeve length cannot be read from the current image is more actionable than a general request for better pictures. Do not approve an unresolved mismatch simply to make a delivery estimate look achievable.

## Parcel submission is another decision point

After selecting warehouse items, check the parcel contents, destination, packaging request, route eligibility and payment state. A saved parcel draft is not a carrier handover. Likewise, a tracking number can exist before the carrier receives the parcel. The event that starts a route's stated transit estimate should be identified in the current service description rather than inferred from the number appearing.

Compare the packing plan with the [shipping cost guide](/articles/kakobuy-shipping-cost-estimate/). A bulky jacket, a shoebox or several protective layers can change the parcel dimensions and available options. Resolving those choices early helps avoid last-minute revisions. It does not make a particular processing speed guaranteed, and removing protection is not automatically the best choice for every item.

## Read the route estimate with its conditions

A transit range is meaningful only alongside the selected route, destination, measurement basis and stated exclusions. Look for whether the range uses working days or calendar days, what event starts the clock, and whether destination processing is included. When those details are not stated clearly, keep the uncertainty visible in your plan instead of treating the shortest number as an arrival commitment.

Route names can also hide different handovers. One company may handle collection, another the international leg and a local carrier the final delivery. Check the tracking record before assuming that a change of carrier or reference means the parcel is lost. Use the [tracking guide](/articles/kakobuy-tracking-purchase-order-parcel/) to distinguish purchase references from international shipment references and to keep the supporting evidence together.

## Build a deadline plan without inventing precision

Start with the date on which you need the goods and work backwards through the unfinished stages. An international estimate alone cannot account for a seller that has not dispatched, an unresolved return or a parcel that has not been paid. Include a personal contingency buffer for uncertainty, and label it as your planning choice rather than an official Kakobuy service promise.

For illustration, imagine that three items are ready while a fourth has no confirmed seller dispatch. There is no sound basis for giving the complete four-item parcel a precise arrival date. Compare two plans: wait for the last item, or request current options for the ready items. Record the additional cost and inconvenience of splitting alongside the timing uncertainty. The useful result is a reasoned choice, not a confident date unsupported by evidence.

## Evaluate a tracking pause in context

The same gap between scans can mean different things at different stages. A label with no acceptance event needs a different inquiry from a parcel that has reached the destination country. Begin with the last confirmed event, its date, the named carrier and the next expected handover. A map or progress bar is less useful than that concrete event history.

When asking for help, send the relevant order or parcel reference privately, the latest status, the date it appeared and a clear description of what you need confirmed. Remove addresses, phone numbers and full tracking references from any public screenshot. Repeatedly posting vague complaints does not provide the missing operational facts. Keep responses in one record so you can see whether the next promised update actually arrives.

## Compare delivery reports on equal terms

A public haul report can illustrate one journey, but it is not a forecast for yours. Check whether its author measured from purchase payment, warehouse submission or carrier acceptance. Look for the destination, route, parcel characteristics and dates. A report missing those details cannot reliably answer how long your shipment should take, even if the writer had a positive experience.

Avoid calculating a platform-wide average from a handful of selected posts. Successful deliveries are not the whole population, and unresolved parcels have no final delivery time yet. For your own completed parcels, record the same stage boundaries each time. That produces a small but interpretable personal history, with limitations, instead of mixing incompatible experiences into one apparently precise number.

## Keep storage deadlines visible while waiting

Waiting for another item does not stop the storage clock. Kakobuy's publicly indexed help describes a normal 100-day storage period starting when an order is marked in storage. Different situations can have different rules, so confirm the actual deadline in the account and read the [warehouse storage guide](/articles/warehouse-storage-and-returns/) before deciding to keep waiting. Storage allowance and seller return eligibility are separate matters.

The best shipping-time record fits on a small worksheet: item or parcel reference, current stage, last confirmed event, unresolved action, current route estimate and personal deadline. Update it when an event changes. This makes the next decision clear, whether that decision is to review QC, clarify dispatch, compare a split parcel or ask for a specific tracking update.
