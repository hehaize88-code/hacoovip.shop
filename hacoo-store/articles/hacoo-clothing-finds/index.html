Hacoo Clothing Finds: Hoodie, T-Shirt and Jacket Size Checks

Choose a clothing category, compare flat garment measurements and see how to check a hoodie, T-shirt or jacket for the cut and layers you want.

Hacoo Store Guide · Updated November 2, 2026

Browse the category → Clothing

Choose a category and a reference garment

Start in the hoodie, T-shirt or jacket category if you want to compare several garments. A single product link is a shortcut to one item and may reopen with a different colour selected. Decide what job the garment has to do before saving it. A hoodie you want to layer under a coat needs a different shoulder and sleeve shape from one you plan to wear over a thick sweatshirt. A T-shirt for tucking in puts more emphasis on body length than one you want to wear loose.

Choose something from your wardrobe that already works for that purpose. It is worth getting the actual garment out instead of relying on a size you remember buying. Look at where it sits at the hem, how the sleeves end and whether you want the new item to be wider or shorter. Record the material and whether it has stretched or shrunk with wear. A favourite washed cotton tee still gives you a useful target for the fit you enjoy, even if its dimensions have changed since you bought it.

Put the seller's numbers and yours on the same basis

Lay your reference garment on a flat surface and smooth obvious folds without stretching the fabric. For chest width, measure straight across between the underarm points. For body length, choose a starting point and record it, such as the high shoulder beside the collar down to the hem. Measure the shoulder seam to seam if the garment has visible shoulder seams. For a sleeve, note whether you began at the shoulder seam, collar or centre back. A useful note contains the endpoints as well as the number.

Now look for the diagram or instructions on the listing. A measurement of 56 cm across a sweatshirt lying flat is not a body chest measurement. Doubling it gives an approximate garment circumference at that point. It still describes the garment and includes the space its cut provides. Do not compare that doubled figure directly with a suggested body-chest range unless the listing explains the relationship. If the chart gives inches and you measured centimetres, convert once, record both units and keep the original number for checking.

Imagine your reference hoodie measures 56 cm across the chest and 66 cm from the high shoulder to the hem. A candidate measured by the same method is 59 cm across and 62 cm long. It would give you about 6 cm more circumference at chest level and 4 cm less length. That might be exactly right over high-waisted trousers and a poor match if you wanted more coverage. Calling the new hoodie “larger” misses the decision. It is wider and shorter by different amounts.

For a hoodie, check the hem and the shoulders

A thick-looking hood and a large chest measurement can make a hoodie look roomy in a photograph. Check where the shoulder seam sits and how the sleeve measurement was taken. A dropped seam moves the starting point down the arm, so a shorter shoulder-to-cuff measurement can still give a long overall reach. If you own a hoodie with a similar shoulder, take a collar-to-cuff measurement from both when the listing permits it. If the methods differ, write down the uncertainty before choosing a size.

Look closely at the hem. A close ribbed band can make a wide hoodie sit at the waist, while a looser hem can fall over the hips. For a zip style, inspect the fastening and pocket position on the selected colour. If you expect to wear it open, consider how the panels hang in the available photographs. For a pullover, check the hood opening and neckline. When warmth or weight matters, look for a stated composition or fabric weight. Studio lighting and the thickness of a folded pile tell you little about how the garment feels in use.

For a T-shirt, look past the graphic

Compare chest width, body length and sleeve opening as a set. Two T-shirts with an identical width can fit very differently if one has a long narrow sleeve and the other ends loosely above the elbow. Check a front and rear view for the placement of side seams, graphic and neckline. A graphic shown separately on a plain background may tell you the intended design without showing the size or position of the print on the garment you would receive.

Look for photographs taken in the selected colour. An image of a dark option gives limited information about the opacity of a white one. If fabric composition or care matters, find the stated description or a readable label for that item. An image of a bright print cannot establish wash durability. A buyer photograph taken after several washes can contribute one person's experience if it identifies the same variant and explains how it was washed. Save that detail separately from the seller's care instructions. If the image supplies neither size nor context, use it for visual reference only.

For a jacket, test the layer you intend to wear

Put on your existing jacket over the kind of garment you plan to wear underneath. Close it, reach forwards and bend your arms. Notice where movement is restricted. A snug upper arm, short sleeve or tight shoulder each points to a different measurement to investigate. Measure the reference jacket while it lies flat, using the selected listing's method where possible. Outer chest width alone cannot establish the usable room inside a lined or padded jacket. If you need room for a sweatshirt, ask about the relevant dimension on the finished garment.

Inspect the closure, pockets and lining on the actual colour option. A flap in the front view can cover a working pocket, a shallow opening or decoration. If you need a secure pocket for a particular object, ask for the usable opening and depth. A detachable hood should have a fastening or description that supports the claim. A jacket photographed in snow may be styled for winter while offering no tested temperature or waterproof rating. Base the choice on the details the listing actually gives and on how you expect to use the jacket.

Make a shortlist you can return to

Keep each candidate's final URL, selected size and colour, measurement method, price and one deciding question. For two black jackets with similar fronts, label one by its stitched cuffs and the other by its button cuffs. These details help when a gallery resets or an old link opens a general feed. If the listing points to a different shopper, seller or checkout site after a redirect, confirm the actual garment before transferring information from your earlier notes.

When two items look equally suitable, compare what remains unknown. A hoodie with a clearly defined width and length may still leave its sleeve method unresolved. A second hoodie may offer better sleeve measurements but have an unconfirmed hem width. If you care most about the sleeve covering your wrist when cycling, the first seller's missing measurement takes priority. Ask a specific question with endpoints and units. A request for the measurement from the base of the collar to the cuff on size M gives a clearer answer than asking whether the seller thinks it runs large.

Check the selected garment through to checkout

Reopen the chosen product and check whether changing colour alters the size menu, material wording, price or gallery. Read the current total with the destination and delivery option you would use. A product found through a Hacoo clothing spreadsheet can open an independent catalogue. Read the order and returns information belonging to the merchant that will take the payment. If you are ordering inside the Hacoo app, use that order's checkout details and keep its confirmation for later support.

Finally, compare the basket description against your shortlist one line at a time. Make sure the garment type, option, quantity and size refer to the item you measured for. Save the relevant size chart with the purchase details while it is still available. If a jacket arrives with a different label, you will have the exact order to compare it with. If the softer blue T-shirt was merely a picture from another colourway, you can catch the mismatch now. A few minutes spent on the selected variant matter more than adding another promising photograph to your list.
