What changes when the parcel grows from 2 kg to 5 kg?

A buyer in Canada has two options. Send the clothes already in the warehouse, or wait for another order and send everything together. The second parcel will be heavier, but a useful budget needs more than a rate per kilogram. On 3 November 2026 we compared two example cartons in JadeShip’s Superbuy calculator. The smaller carton weighed 2 kg and measured 30 × 25 × 15 cm. The larger weighed 5 kg and measured 40 × 30 × 22 cm. Both scenarios assumed ordinary unbranded clothing travelling from Guangdong to Canada.

On the Ocean Route (Special), the larger carton added CNY 105 to the displayed estimate. The difference on each of the two faster lines shown above was CNY 278. Those are meaningful amounts when deciding whether to add several garments to a shipment. They describe two complete example parcels, including a change in dimensions. They do not isolate the price of each extra kilogram. Keep the original quote if you want to measure what one particular garment does to your own shipment.

Enter the packed parcel, down to the units

Superbuy’s official estimator asks for destination, warehouse, weight in grams, outer dimensions in centimetres and item category. Enter 2,000 for the smaller parcel and 5,000 for the larger. A label showing the weight of a shirt gives you a starting point. The weight you need before paying for freight includes the other garments, protective material and outer packaging. If the warehouse has not packed the items together yet, write down which measurements are estimates so you remember to replace them.

Measure the longest outside edges of the sealed carton. Two folded sweaters can have the same scale weight as two sweatshirts yet occupy different amounts of space. A rigid presentation box can take up space even when the garment compresses easily. Before discarding it, decide whether it protects an item, belongs with a gift or simply keeps the seller’s original presentation intact. If you change packing, rerun all three dimensions and the weight together. Adjusting just the grams leaves the quote attached to the old box.

Size matters for a second reason. These two cartons occupy 11,250 and 26,400 cubic centimetres respectively. Some services convert volume into billable weight using their own rules. Before using a formula, open the conditions of the specific line and look up its divisor, rounding and any size limits. If they are missing, do not infer them from the two quotes above. Enter the actual dimensions in the estimator. Keep the scale weight nearby so you can identify why a lightly packed garment has a surprisingly high quote.

Read the three services against your own deadline

In this comparison the ocean service gave the lowest displayed price and the longest estimated transit range. For a buyer stocking up on basics, the extra time might be acceptable. For a coat wanted for a particular trip, compare the two faster options first. The apparel line was CNY 13 below the general air service at both weights. Their published time ranges overlapped. A buyer paying for the air service solely because its name sounds faster would want to check those ranges before choosing.

A transit estimate is only one part of the timeline from checkout to delivery. Think about when the seller can dispatch, when the last item can reach the warehouse, and how long you can wait for packing before the international leg begins. Keep the carrier’s displayed estimate separate from those earlier stages. If timing matters, record the latest acceptable dispatch day and the practical consequence of a late arrival. A cheap line that would make a garment arrive after your trip has a different value for you.

For a budget worksheet you can divide each displayed amount by the parcel’s scale weight. On the ocean service that arithmetic gives CNY 65.50 per kg for the smaller example and CNY 47.20 per kg for the larger one. These are averages for the two selected cartons. Applying either average to a third carton can be misleading. A service may have a minimum charge or bill additional weight in steps. A small change can leave the price unchanged or move it into the next band.

Separate freight from what you have already paid

JadeShip displayed these estimates with its payment-fee setting at 3%, its service fee and shipping adjustment at zero, and the welcome bonus switched off. Those are comparison settings. They cannot tell you which payment charges or discounts apply to your account. Keeping both runs in CNY also avoids an apparent price difference caused by changing exchange rates. If you budget in Canadian dollars, note the conversion date and use your payment provider’s rate to translate your chosen estimate.

For an order budget, make separate entries for the goods, domestic delivery to the warehouse, optional services and international shipping. Then compare the last entry with the amount displayed when submitting the actual parcel. The examples above already include the calculator’s chosen settings. Adding another blanket 3% to those numbers would count the same assumption twice. Read the route details for what the quoted service covers, even when its name includes “Duty-Prepaid”. Any amount payable separately should have its own entry and a source rather than being silently folded into the freight figure.

Test consolidation without paying for a second parcel

Suppose the 2 kg group is ready but the remaining items are still with a seller. To compare sending two parcels against one 5 kg shipment, you need a third estimate for the remaining items packed separately. Subtracting the smaller quote from the larger tells you the difference between those two examples. It does not tell you what it costs to ship the remaining items in their own packaging. A second carton may incur another minimum charge, have different dimensions or qualify for a different route.

Build both options from the same destination and item descriptions. For the split option, add the two independent quotes and record any extra packing costs. For the combined option, keep one quote based on the proposed outer carton. Then note the non-price trade-off. Sending the first parcel sooner lets you use some items earlier. Waiting can simplify delivery and reduce packaging, but it also leaves the first items in storage for longer. Check each item’s remaining storage and return options before extending the wait for a cheaper shipment.

One additional comparison is worth making when the second order contains a single bulky piece. Price the smaller parcel with that piece packed more closely, while keeping enough protection for the fabric and closures. If the amount stays the same, a change of packaging alone offers little reason to delay the shipment.

Repeat the comparison when your warehouse record changes

Start with the nearest example above, replace every assumption you know, then run the official Superbuy estimator for your actual destination and item category. If a service disappears, examine the category, destination, packed size and weight before comparing prices. Adding a branded accessory, a battery-powered item or a bottle to ordinary clothes changes the question. Keep the contents accurate and let the available routes change. If the address or contents need clarification, resolve them before selecting a line.

Save the date, route name, displayed currency, weight, dimensions and quoted amount together. At parcel submission, compare the deposit with your saved estimate and read the selected service terms again. Superbuy’s forwarding guide explains that the carrier’s verified measurements are used to settle the final freight. Once the parcel is dispatched, save that final charge beside the estimate. The pair of records gives you a practical reference for the next similar order and enough detail to ask about a difference if one appears.
