Hipobuy Sneaker Sizing: EU, US and Insole Measurements

Separate shoe labels, foot length and insole measurements, then inspect both shoes before approving a warehouse order.

Scope note

This independent guide explains a decision process, not a guaranteed rate, delivery date, tax result or refund outcome.

01

Treat the size label as a starting point

Hipobuy sneaker sizing depends on the exact listing and version you select. An EU or US label is a useful starting point, but it is not a universal promise of fit across brands, models or sellers. The shape of the shoe, available width and internal construction can matter as much as a nominal size. Begin with your own measurements and a comparable pair that fits. Keep the chosen listing and size chart together so evidence from another model does not accidentally become the basis for this order.

Before paying, establish which size system the selector uses. US men's, US women's, UK and EU labels are distinct systems. A number without its system can be ambiguous, especially when a catalogue combines adult and youth options. Do not infer the intended system from your location or from the first number visible in a photograph. Check the selected option, chart and written order note. If these disagree, resolve the discrepancy first. A clear record of the requested size is essential when you later compare the received label at the warehouse.

02

Measure both feet consistently

Measure your feet on a firm, flat surface using a method appropriate to the chart you intend to follow. Record the heel-to-toe distance and repeat the measurement for the other foot. A helper can reduce errors caused by bending and shifting your weight while trying to mark the endpoint. Keep the paper and measuring tool stable, and write down the unit. Small differences between measurements should prompt a careful repeat rather than an immediate jump to a different shoe size.

Official fitting instructions can help you understand the method, but they apply to the chart and products described by that brand. Nike's official footwear guide distinguishes measured foot length from the CM size printed on labels and boxes. That is an important reason not to treat every number followed by cm as the same measurement. Use the corresponding chart for the exact product when it is available. A seller's claim that a model follows another brand's sizing should be treated as a claim to verify, not as a substitute for useful evidence about the received pair.

03

Keep foot length, insole length and outsole length separate

Foot length describes your foot. Insole length describes a removable or visible component inside a shoe. Outsole length describes the exterior bottom. These measurements answer different questions and should not be substituted for one another. An outsole can extend beyond the usable interior. An insole can curve upward or have edges that do not represent the space your foot occupies. A photograph of the sole next to a ruler therefore cannot automatically determine the correct size for your foot.

If a removable insole can be measured without damage, request a view that shows its full length and the tape endpoints. State whether the tape should follow a curve or use a straight line, then compare with a reference measured the same way. Do not prescribe one universal extra centimetre allowance for every sneaker. Toe shape, socks, intended activity and personal fit preferences vary. Treat the measurement as additional evidence alongside the label, chart and comparable footwear. Where the construction prevents a reliable measurement, acknowledge that limit instead of converting an uncertain photograph into a precise recommendation.

04

Compare the relevant dimensions of a familiar pair

Choose reference footwear with a similar purpose and shape. A roomy casual sneaker may not be a useful comparison for a narrow performance shoe. Record the model, marked size, width designation if present and how it fits you. Note whether your concern is toe pressure, heel movement, forefoot width or overall length. A general statement that a pair feels small is less useful than a description of where it does not fit. These notes help identify the evidence you need from the new listing.

For an illustrative comparison, suppose two removable insoles have the same measured length but different widths near the forefoot. The identical length does not make their shoes equivalent. Likewise, two shoes with the same EU label can have different internal shapes. Do not invent a new conversion table from these observations. Instead, ask whether the exact variation has a chart or measurement that addresses your concern. If the listing offers no meaningful information and returns are difficult, the uncertainty itself belongs in your purchase decision. You do not have to turn every attractive photograph into an order.

05

Verify both shoes when the pair arrives

Begin warehouse QC with identity checks. Compare the order record, selected colour, version and size labels on both shoes. A matching box label is not enough if the individual shoes are labelled differently. Request a photograph of each label if the standard pictures do not show them. Check that the received pair matches the chosen option rather than another style from a multi-option listing. Keep the product ID visible in your saved notes so photographs from different orders are not confused.

Then inspect the pair together from useful angles: front, back, outer sides, inner sides and soles where available. Look for visible damage, large asymmetry, loose components, unusual marks or missing included accessories. Compare like-for-like angles before judging shape. A shoe positioned closer to the camera can appear larger. Ask for a clearer view if perspective makes a material difference difficult to assess. Photographs can document visible construction and identity, but they cannot certify authenticity, cushioning performance, comfort or long-term durability. Those limits should remain clear even when the visual checks are satisfactory.

06

Resolve a size concern before international shipping

If the received label is wrong, provide the selected option and a clear photograph of the mismatch. If the label is correct but the measurement differs from the listing, describe the exact dimension and measuring method. Ask the relevant service what exchange or return options remain under the current terms. Avoid claiming a manufacturing error solely because a third-party size chart differs. A specific evidence-based request is easier to investigate than a general complaint that the size looks strange.

Keep three decisions separate: whether the seller sent the ordered option, whether the received item matches the published measurements, and whether it is likely to fit your needs. The first two concern the order evidence; the third can retain uncertainty even when the listing was accurate. Record any additional domestic delivery, service or return costs before replacing the pair. An agent's warehouse storage period does not automatically extend a seller's return window. Handle the concern while the practical choices are still available, and do not approve international dispatch merely to avoid asking for one useful measurement.

07

Plan packaging after approving the item

Once the pair is acceptable, decide what packaging you need. Keeping a shoe box may protect presentation and storage convenience, while removing it may alter parcel volume. Neither choice guarantees a particular freight saving or damage outcome. Ask which protective options are actually available and compare the final packed dimensions for eligible routes. Do not assume that the lightest arrangement is suitable for every shoe. Item approval and parcel protection solve different problems and deserve separate instructions.

Save the final order record, labels, measurement photos and packing choice. If you later buy a similar model, these observations will be more useful than a remembered size letter or a general comment from another buyer. Start future searches from the shoe category, then repeat the same checks for the new variation. This workflow cannot guarantee fit from a distance, but it gives each decision a clear basis. You know which numbers describe your feet, which describe the item, and which details still depend on trying the shoes on after delivery.
