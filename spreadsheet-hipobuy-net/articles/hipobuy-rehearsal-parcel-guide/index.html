Scope note

This independent guide explains a decision process, not a guaranteed rate, delivery date, tax result or refund outcome.

01

Establish what the rehearsal service includes

Hipobuy's official homepage currently announces a Rehearsal Parcel feature. Before using it, read the current explanation in your account or ask official support what the service includes for your order. The practical question is whether you can obtain more useful packing information before committing to international shipping. This guide does not claim a fixed rehearsal price, completion time, refund policy or guaranteed saving. Those details were not established by the public announcement and should be confirmed where the service is offered.

Separate the rehearsal request from a final shipping instruction. Confirm which items will be included, what packing work will be performed, what measurements or photographs you will receive, and whether further changes create another charge. Ask whether the result is an estimate or the configuration that will actually be dispatched. Keep the answer with your parcel notes. A service name alone does not tell you whether every route is supported, whether an existing quote will be recalculated automatically, or whether a later change of contents will require another measurement.

02

Finish item decisions before requesting packing work

Create a parcel list containing only the items you intend to send together. For each one, confirm the selected variation, quantity, warehouse status and any unresolved QC question. An item awaiting a size measurement or a seller return should not silently become part of an approved parcel. Resolve those decisions first when possible. Repacking after removing or adding an item can change weight, dimensions, protective materials and route eligibility, making an earlier rehearsal result less useful for the shipment you eventually choose.

Keep item approval and packing approval as separate checks. Warehouse photographs may help establish that a garment matches your selection, while parcel photographs answer questions about how the contents were arranged and protected. Neither set replaces the other. Write instructions that identify the relevant item or order, especially when several similar products are stored together. If a particular box, strap or accessory must remain, state that before packing. The person preparing a mixed parcel should not have to infer which components matter to your purchase.

03

Understand actual and volumetric weight

Actual weight is the measured mass of the packed parcel. Volumetric weight is a calculation based on its outer dimensions and a divisor specified by the selected service. A route may use actual weight, dimensional weight, the larger of the two, or another published charging method. Read that route's terms before using any formula. A generic calculator cannot choose the applicable divisor, minimum charge, rounding rule or size limit for you. The same physical carton can therefore produce different chargeable results under different service rules.

For a route that uses length multiplied by width multiplied by height divided by a stated divisor, use the outer packed dimensions and the required units. Do not combine centimetres with a divisor intended for another unit system. Keep the measured values at their original precision and apply the carrier's rounding instructions separately. The weight planner on this site displays a simple comparison from your inputs. It is useful for understanding the relationship between size and weight, but its displayed number is not a final carrier charge or a live Hipobuy quotation.

04

Work through a clearly hypothetical parcel comparison

Consider an illustrative parcel weighing 3.2 kg with outer dimensions of 40 × 30 × 25 cm. If, solely for this example, the chosen route uses a divisor of 6,000 and charges the greater of actual and volumetric weight, the dimensional calculation is 40 × 30 × 25 ÷ 6,000 = 5.0 kg. Before any route rounding or minimum applies, the comparison weight is therefore 5.0 kg. These dimensions, weight and divisor are invented teaching inputs, not a measured Hipobuy parcel or a published rate for a particular route.

Now imagine suitable repacking reduces the outer dimensions to 35 × 28 × 20 cm while the actual weight remains 3.2 kg. The same illustrative divisor gives approximately 3.27 kg of volumetric weight. That reduces the raw comparison from 5.0 kg to about 3.27 kg, but it does not establish a monetary saving. The route may round weight, use pricing steps or have a minimum charge; packing services may cost extra. Compare the final payable quotes for both configurations, including those additional charges, before deciding which option is better.

  • Example A: 40 × 30 × 25 cm; 3.2 kg actual; 5.0 kg calculated volume weight.
  • Example B: 35 × 28 × 20 cm; 3.2 kg actual; approximately 3.27 kg calculated volume weight.
  • Illustrative divisor: 6,000; use it only when the actual route specifies that value.
  • No freight price is inferred: route pricing, rounding and service costs remain separate.
05

Give packing instructions that protect the intended contents

Describe the result you need for each type of item. A structured bag may need support to preserve its shape, shoes may need protection against crushing, and a printed garment may need care around folded decorations. These considerations can conflict with minimizing carton volume. Avoid a blanket instruction to remove all packaging unless you understand what will be discarded and how the remaining contents will be protected. If a packing method is offered, ask whether it is suitable for your goods and how it affects compensation or service conditions.

Use an itemized instruction list rather than several contradictory messages. Specify what to retain, what may be removed and what requires clarification. Ask for confirmation before an irreversible change such as discarding an accessory or original packaging you wanted to keep. Do not assume compression is appropriate for every garment or product. An attractive dimensional reduction loses its value if it creates avoidable damage or removes something you paid for. Judge the proposed configuration by both its measurable shipping effect and its suitability for the actual contents.

06

Compare eligible quotes for the same finished parcel

Once measurements are available, record the destination postcode, contents, declared value, packed dimensions and measured weight above your route comparison. Use those same inputs for every candidate service. Check eligibility before looking at price: a route that cannot carry the goods or accept the parcel size is not an alternative. Then record freight, separately charged packing or services, the quoted delivery range, tracking scope and any stated restrictions. Preserve the quote date because route availability and amounts can change between your initial estimate and payment.

Check where each cost appears and avoid counting it twice. Hipobuy's fee explanation treats international freight separately from product and domestic delivery costs; optional work may create another line in your own budget. A coupon can affect only particular charges, so compare the actual payable totals under the displayed terms. If packing changes after the quote, request an updated comparison rather than continuing to use the old number. Keep destination taxes and handling questions visible where relevant, and confirm the actual collection arrangement without assuming a shipping label guarantees a particular customs outcome.

07

Approve a documented parcel and keep the evidence

Before final shipping payment, match the parcel list to the approved items and review any supplied packing evidence. Check that required pieces were retained, unresolved items were excluded and the address is correct. Save the measured weight, dimensions, selected route, final quote and important conditions together. If the final weight or price differs from an earlier estimate, identify the changed input or charging rule. A precise comparison is more useful than assuming that every difference is an error or that every rehearsal must produce a lower bill.

After dispatch, keep the international parcel and carrier identifiers alongside the earlier item records. They document a different stage of the same purchase and help you distinguish warehouse work from carrier movement. If an issue arises, provide the relevant measurements, packing instructions, photos and dates through official support. For your next parcel, reuse the record as a method, not as a universal price table. Rehearsal packing is most useful when it turns uncertain assumptions into a clearly described parcel that you can compare and approve with confidence about what the numbers actually mean.
