Jersey spreadsheet guide

Kakobuy Jersey Spreadsheet: Sizing and QC Checklist

Use a Kakobuy jersey spreadsheet without guessing: verify the live listing, compare garment measurements, and inspect names, numbers, badges and seams.

By Kakobuys.shop Research Desk12 min read1,636 wordsFact-checked August 9, 2026

A Kakobuy jersey spreadsheet is useful for discovery, but it cannot tell you whether a particular shirt will fit, whether the seller changed a season option, or whether the name set on the warehouse item is aligned. Treat every row as a lead. The decision should come from the live listing, the exact variant you ordered and the quality-control evidence attached to that warehouse item.

Kakobuy’s current public process says users can submit product links, receive warehouse quality inspection and select stored items for one international parcel. That describes the stages of the service; it does not make a spreadsheet row current or prove jersey quality. This guide supplies the missing verification layer for football, basketball and American-football jerseys without inventing a universal size conversion or photo count.

Fast ruleRecord the team, season, cut, colour, size, player/name-set option and source URL before payment. At the warehouse, verify identity first, measurements second and decoration third.

Use the spreadsheet to shortlist, not to approve

A useful jersey row should identify more than a team. Look for the season, home/away/third designation, player or blank-back option, size range, listed price, seller link and the date the row was checked. If the row says only “Manchester jersey,” it cannot distinguish several colours, sponsors, cuts or years.

Open the destination rather than relying on the spreadsheet thumbnail. Confirm that it is a product page, not a search result or login redirect. Compare the seller, title, option menu and current price with the saved row. If the exact season or name-set choice has disappeared, do not select the nearest-looking replacement. Mark the row unavailable or find a clearer listing.

Define the exact jersey before choosing a size

“Jersey” covers different constructions. A supporter-style shirt may have a roomier cut and applied graphics; a player-style version may be closer to the body and use different fabric or decoration. Basketball jerseys can be sleeveless and measured differently from football shirts. Youth, women’s and adult labels are not interchangeable.

Write one specification line: “2024 away, adult supporter cut, navy, blank back, seller size L.” Keep original option text or model codes beside the translation. This line becomes the reference for the purchase order and warehouse check. Without it, a correct team in the wrong cut can look superficially acceptable.

Size from garment measurements, not letters

Do not convert a seller’s L into your usual L by habit. Measure a jersey you already own and like while it lies flat. Record pit-to-pit width, body length from the high shoulder point and, for sleeved shirts, sleeve length or shoulder width. Compare those numbers with the seller’s chart using the same units and measurement method.

For example, suppose your reference jersey measures 54 cm pit to pit and 72 cm long. One seller option lists 52 × 70 cm and the next lists 55 × 73 cm. The second is numerically closer, but that is a decision example, not a recommendation: fabric stretch, cut and measurement tolerance still matter. Choose the acceptable range before ordering, such as width 53–56 cm and length 71–74 cm.

Read option prices before checkout

The lowest visible price may belong to a child size, blank version, patch-only option or another variation. Select the exact size and decoration, then check the final merchandise subtotal. Record whether a name set or sleeve patch is included, optional or unavailable. Do not infer inclusion from the hero photo.

If the imported option names are shortened or translated ambiguously, use a concise buyer note with original text and a stop condition: “navy away shirt, adult supporter cut, size L, blank back; no player print and no substitution.” A testable instruction is more useful than “best version” or “same as photo.”

Inspect warehouse photos in a fixed order

First confirm identity: front and back colour blocking, collar, sleeve shape, sponsor position and the ordered name/number. Then check shape: the body should lie evenly, side seams should not twist visibly and corresponding panels should be reasonably symmetrical. Only after those checks should you zoom in on badges, print edges and stitching.

QC photos can show visible construction, but they cannot prove fabric durability, exact colour under every light, long-term print adhesion or authenticity. A folded hem or camera angle can also imitate unevenness. Ask for one targeted extra view when it could change your decision instead of requesting generic additional photos.

Name and number alignment

Use stable references such as the collar centre, shoulder seams and side edges. Check whether a name is centred relative to the number and whether multi-digit numbers share a baseline and spacing. Perspective can distort vertical lines, so compare several edges rather than judging one diagonal photo.

Inspect the perimeter of heat-applied decoration for lifting, bubbles, creases or material trapped beneath it. A photo may reveal a present defect; it cannot guarantee how the print will behave after washing. If lettering is customised, confirm the spelling against the written order rather than memory.

Badges, sponsors and patches

Check that the ordered patches are present and placed consistently with the listing version. Compare left/right position against seams, not just the shirt centre. Look for obvious puckering, loose edges, excess backing or a badge rotated relative to a nearby panel.

Different seasons or competition versions can legitimately use different sponsors and patches. That is why the saved source option matters: QC should answer “does this match the ordered listing?” rather than “does this match a random reference photo?”

Collar, hems and panel seams

Trace the collar join, shoulder seams, sleeve hems and bottom hem. Look for missed stitches, open seams, heavy puckering and abrupt changes in stitch path. Mesh panels should meet at the intended seam without a large offset. Minor loose threads may be removable; an open structural seam is a different risk.

Compare both sides when the construction should be symmetrical. A shirt laid carelessly can look uneven, so request it flattened if the decision depends on shape. Keep the question narrow: “please lay flat and measure pit to pit” produces stronger evidence than “please check quality.”

Request measurements that answer a decision

If size is the main uncertainty, ask for a ruler photo of pit-to-pit width or body length and specify the endpoints. Compare the result with your preselected acceptable range. A measurement outside that range is actionable; a label photo repeating “L” is not.

Allow for reasonable measurement variation and fabric position. Do not manufacture precision from a curved garment or unclear ruler angle. If a one-centimetre difference determines acceptance, your original fit range may be too narrow for this kind of purchase.

Decide: accept, clarify or request after-sales help

Accept when the item identity, measurements and visible construction fall within the limits you wrote before purchase. Clarify when the evidence is inconclusive—for example, a fold hides a number edge or the ruler endpoints are unclear. Seek the applicable return or after-sales option when the item conflicts with the order or a visible defect exceeds your threshold.

Act from the current order page because seller eligibility and deadlines can vary. Kakobuy’s publicly indexed product help currently says users may apply for a return within five days when dissatisfied, with the buyer service negotiating seller after-sales. Treat that as a current public description, not a promise that every customised or seller-restricted jersey is eligible. Save the order state, issue photo and request date.

Plan the parcel without damaging the decision

Soft jerseys usually consolidate more easily than rigid goods, but decoration can be creased by aggressive compression. Decide whether patches, printed names or delicate presentation need a fold barrier or looser packing. Separate protection needs from assumptions about a cheaper parcel.
