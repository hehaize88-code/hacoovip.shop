Footwear QC

Kakobuy Shoes Spreadsheet: A QC Checklist That Goes Beyond the Front Photo

Use a Kakobuy shoes spreadsheet to discover products, then verify the live route, size system, pair symmetry, sole line and measurements before parcel submission.

By Kakobuys.shop Research Desk12 min read1,281 wordsReviewed July 30, 2026

A shoes spreadsheet is useful for discovery, but it cannot make the buying decision for you. The row can tell you where a product link pointed on a review date. It cannot guarantee that the seller, selected batch, size option or price is unchanged. A footwear workflow therefore has two jobs: confirm the destination first, then compare the actual warehouse pair in a repeatable order.

The order matters. Buyers often zoom into stitching before checking that both shoes have the same overall shape or that the label uses the expected size system. A neat close-up of the wrong size is not useful. Start with identity and pair-level geometry, then move toward small construction details.

Footwear review orderLive source → selected model and size → both shoes together → heel and toe symmetry → sole bond and level → labels and measurements → visible defects → current after-sales decision.

What a trustworthy shoe row should show

A useful row contains a descriptive product name, current destination, source or marketplace item ID when available, representative image, category, review date and a direct route to an independent detail page. It should not claim “best quality” simply because an old community post was positive. Prices and seller pages change, so the row should encourage a live recheck.

Image-source consistency matters. A generic stock sneaker photo may look polished but does not tell a searcher what appears at the destination. This catalog uses representative first images from the corresponding main-site product records and names the product in the image alternative text. The image is a navigation clue, not QC proof.

Confirm the model and ordered size before judging quality

Compare the warehouse record with the exact source option saved at purchase. Check colour blocking, model code, size label and quantity. Multi-colour listings can reuse similar cover images, and a seller may use separate sizing for different models. If the source page changed, use the order-date evidence rather than today’s default option.

Do not translate a number without identifying its system. EU 42, US 9, UK 8 and CN labels are not interchangeable by the printed number alone. Compare the seller’s chart and request an insole or outsole measurement when conversion is uncertain. A box label supports identification but does not confirm internal fit.

Put both shoes in the same orientation

The strongest first QC image shows the pair together, camera centred and shoes aligned. Compare toe-box width, side profile, collar height, tongue height and the rear heel line. One shoe should not sit visibly higher, lean differently or show a substantially different toe curve. Use table lines or the measurement mat to detect camera perspective.

A small difference may come from stuffing, lacing or angle. Ask for a straight repeat when the apparent asymmetry changes between images. A problem that remains visible in front, rear and top views is stronger evidence than one distorted frame.

Follow the sole as one continuous line

Inspect the lateral and medial edges and the rear. Look for gaps, excessive adhesive, a tilted outsole or a tread edge that sits differently between the pair. Follow the join from toe to heel instead of judging one isolated spot. Lighting can make clear glue look white, so request a closer neutral view when the line is ambiguous.

Check that the sole pattern and colour match the selected model. A photograph can reveal visible separation or misalignment, but it cannot predict long-term bond strength, cushioning performance or slip resistance. Keep those limits explicit.

Use reference points for panels and artwork

Compare repeated panels, eyelets, overlays and printed or embroidered elements using seams as reference lines. Left and right placement should be reasonably consistent. Check whether the tongue label is centred and whether heel tabs sit at similar heights. For patterned uppers, confirm that the ordered treatment appears on both shoes.

Do not evaluate authenticity from a logo close-up. A visible mark can be compared for position and damage, but photographs do not establish provenance or materials. The relevant decision is whether the visible item matches the ordered specification and your tolerance for construction differences.

Measure the inside when fit is the deciding factor

Insole length is often more useful than a regional label, but the measurement method must be visible. Ask for the tape from the heel end to the longest toe point with the insole flat when removal is permitted. If the insole cannot be removed, an outsole measurement can provide context but is not the same as internal usable length.

Compare the measurement with a similar shoe that fits, measured the same way. Leave room for toe shape and intended fit. A 28 cm insole does not guarantee comfort because width, volume, padding and last shape also matter.

Box removal is a shipping decision, not a default

A shoe box can add dimensional volume. Removing it may reduce a parcel’s outer size, but it also removes structure and some identification. Compare a compact packing scenario with a protected one. If the shoes have a shape that can collapse, request suitable internal support or keep the box if the additional cost is acceptable.

Use the live route’s dimensional rule and packed measurements. Do not claim a fixed saving from box removal because the parcel may remain in the same chargeable step. A packing change matters only when it changes the route’s billed result without creating unacceptable damage risk.

Handle a mismatch while options remain open

If the wrong size or colour arrived, document the order option, label and measurement in one message. Kakobuy’s public help describes a five-day return/exchange guarantee for eligible warehouse orders, subject to seller participation and restrictions. Check the current order state and deadline rather than assuming every shoe can be returned.

For a possible construction defect, mark the image and state the location: “right shoe, lateral sole near heel, visible gap.” Avoid judging the entire product from a blurry frame. Request one decisive view when necessary, then make the after-sales decision before parcel submission.

Ten-point shoe QC checklist

  1. Destination, seller, model and selected variant still match the saved order.
  2. Both shoes and the expected accessories are present.
  3. Colour blocking and model code match the selected option.
  4. Toe shape, collar and heel profile are compared as a pair.
  5. Both sole edges are followed from toe to heel.
  6. Panels, eyelets, tongue labels and heel tabs are compared.
  7. Size system is identified rather than guessed.
  8. Insole or outsole measurement is requested when material.
  9. Visible stains, dents, tears and missing parts are recorded.
  10. Packing protection and dimensional volume are considered together.

A good Kakobuy shoes spreadsheet does not promise a perfect pair. It reduces wasted browsing, keeps the product route current and gives the buyer a category-specific inspection method. The final decision still belongs to the warehouse evidence, current after-sales options and live parcel terms.
