How to Find Kakobuy QC Photos Before You Buy

Look up warehouse photos by the original product link, match the seller’s option and size, and work out what to do if a Kakobuy QC finder returns nothing.

By Kakobuys.shop Research Desk3 November 2026

Start with the item you would actually order

Open the product page, choose the colour and size you want, and copy its link. Paste that link into the independent KakoFind QC Finder. If it returns an album, compare the marketplace, item ID and selected option before using any of the photos to make a decision. If you already placed an order and want to see what arrived for you, go to that order in your Kakobuy account. A public finder searches pictures from earlier purchases.

The difference matters most when a seller offers many versions on one page. A warehouse photograph of a blue hoodie in medium can tell you how that graphic looked on that particular piece. It leaves the measurements of a black hoodie in large unanswered. Decide what you are trying to learn before you search. “Can I read the option label?” and “Has anyone measured this size?” lead to more useful answers than browsing the most attractive album.

Get a product URL that a finder can read

Start from the screen where you can choose a variant. On Weidian, the product number usually follows itemID= in the URL. A Taobao product link commonly carries id=, while a 1688 offer normally has its number in the path after /offer/. Keep the marketplace and the number together. A shop number, basket reference or purchase order number can also be a long string of digits, but it identifies something else.

If a spreadsheet opens an intermediate page, follow it until you see the item and its options. If the address is a shortened share link, open it in a browser and copy the destination product URL. A link to a shop, a screenshot hosted on a photo site or a collection of thirty items gives a finder no clear product to match. Where an agent link cannot be read, try the original seller link for that same item. Only the public product address belongs in the finder.

A real lookup example with two GEL-KAYANO 14 listings

Our ASICS comparison records two marketplace listings described by their sellers as GEL-KAYANO 14. The record with ID 7730289026 described 18 styles at the October review. Record 7706333190 described nine. These two numbers are useful practice because a search for the model name could lead to either listing. Open the comparison, select one record and carry its ID into your search notes. If you move to the other listing, start a second note.

Suppose you are considering a silver pair in EU 42 from the first listing. An album attached to the second listing cannot establish what the first seller sent. Even an album under the first ID needs a second check. Can you identify the silver variant and read the size label? An image with no visible label can help you ask for a better angle, but it cannot settle a sizing decision. Silver and EU 42 are examples for this exercise. Availability and the existence of a matching album have to be checked at the time of your search.

Read the album against one question at a time

Record the photo source, its date if shown, the option and the view that answers your question. For a pair of shoes, a readable tongue label can show the tagged size. To estimate usable length, look for a measurement with both ends of the tape visible and check what was measured. A ruler beside the outside of a shoe measures something different from a ruler along a removable insole. Keep the original centimetres beside any size conversion.

Apply the same reasoning to clothing and accessories. A hoodie photographed flat can show the width between the underarms if both endpoints are visible. A seller’s chest circumference and a flat width are different quantities. For a fitted cap, a clear rear view or internal size label can answer a question that a front logo cannot. Write down the fact visible in the picture and leave the remaining field blank. It is better to request one useful view than to collect six more photographs of the front.

If you find several albums, check whether they show distinct received items. A copied photograph appearing twice adds no new evidence. A recent photo of another colour may be less useful for your decision than an older, well-labelled photo of the exact option. Consider the age of the listing, any changes to its options and the reason you wanted photographs in the first place.

What to do with a rejected link, an empty result or a mismatch

When the finder rejects an address, return to the original product page, check that it still opens and compare the item number before trying again. When it recognises the link but has no photos, record that outcome separately. A public archive may have no accessible album for that item. The empty search alone tells you nothing about how many units were sold, whether a seller is reliable or how your chosen item fits.

A result showing another colour, size or marketplace ID needs a narrower decision. Use it only for details you can genuinely compare. If the missing point is essential, ask for a size chart or a specific view, choose another listing that you can evaluate, or wait for your own warehouse inspection. If you switch sellers, start the identity check again. Even a listing using the same cover image may have a different set of options.

Turn the search into a useful order note

Before paying, save the original product link, marketplace ID, selected option wording, size, date checked and unresolved question. For the first shoe listing, a note could read “7730289026, seller describes GEL-KAYANO 14. Match the chosen silver option. EU 42 requested. Insole length still unknown.” Record an album link only if you found it. That short note remains useful if the seller changes the first photo or renames an option after you have ordered.

When your order reaches the warehouse, return to its own record. Compare the item, quantity, labels and any measurement you requested with the note you made. Choose the next photograph according to the remaining uncertainty. If the labels on a pair do not match, ask for one picture showing both labels together. If a measurement is unreadable, state the endpoints and unit you need. Check the options shown for your order before arranging international delivery.

Keep a short comparison record

Use one row per album. In the first column put the original marketplace and item number. In the next two, copy the option you plan to order and the option actually visible in the album. Leave a blank wherever the photograph or caption gives no answer. In a final column write the one detail you still need from the seller or warehouse. When you revisit the item on your phone, this small record shows exactly which observation supported your choice.

Know when you have enough evidence

A historical photo search has done its job when you can decide whether to order, ask a specific question or pass on a listing. A clear photo of an earlier order can support a choice about visible colour placement or a recorded dimension. Material composition, long-term wear, authenticity and the condition of the item that will be sent to you require other evidence. Where the listing identity itself is uncertain, more close-ups of the same unidentified item will not resolve it.

Use the ASICS comparison to practise matching records, the public finder to look for an earlier album, and Kakobuy’s account area to inspect your own order after arrival. Once you have your own photographs, continue to our guide to reading warehouse QC before shipping. That is the point to assess the pair or garment actually assigned to you.
