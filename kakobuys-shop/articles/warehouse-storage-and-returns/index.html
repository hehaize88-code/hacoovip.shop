Warehouse Storage, Returns and the Five-Day Clock

What Kakobuy’s public help pages say about free storage and eligible returns, plus the checks you should make before relying on a deadline.

By Kakobuys.shop Research Desk8 min readReviewed July 29, 2026

Warehouse deadlines are easy to misunderstand because several clocks can apply to one order. Storage time, seller return eligibility, customer-service reporting and international parcel claims are not the same window. A long storage allowance does not extend a short seller return period.

The public 100-day storage reference

Kakobuy’s publicly indexed inspection and storage information, reviewed July 29, 2026, mentions 100 days of free storage at its warehouse. The same summary encourages buyers to combine more items before international shipping to reduce costs. That can be useful for consolidation, but “free storage” should not be read as “all other options remain open for 100 days.”

Record the warehouse arrival date of each item. If a parcel contains products that arrived on different days, the oldest item can reach its deadline first. Confirm whether the service counts calendar days, how it displays approaching expiry and what happens after the free period. These operating details can change even when an older search result still shows the headline policy.

The five-day return/exchange guarantee is conditional

Kakobuy’s publicly indexed five-day guarantee entry, reviewed July 29, 2026, describes the programme as a special after-sales service offered by sellers on third-party platforms. The key word is eligible. A seller may not participate, a marketplace may restrict a category, or a customised or used item may be excluded. The actual order status is stronger evidence than a general guide.

The five-day clock is important because it can expire long before the free storage period. Inspect warehouse photos soon after arrival. If a measurement, colour or visible defect is unclear, request the additional evidence while an after-sales option may still be available.

A practical arrival-day routine

  1. Open the warehouse record when the arrival notice appears.
  2. Match the item, colour, size and quantity with the saved order.
  3. Review the widest QC photos before looking at small details.
  4. Check measurements when fit or dimensions affect the decision.
  5. Look for the order’s actual return/exchange control and deadline.
  6. Report one visible issue per sentence and attach the relevant photo.
  7. Keep the response and decision with the order record.

Consolidation changes the shipping calculation

Combining warehouse items can reduce duplicated packaging and improve the price per item, but the final charge depends on the line, destination, actual weight, volumetric weight and parcel dimensions. A large light item can make volume more important than scale weight. A restricted product can also reduce the available shipping lines for the whole parcel.

Before submission, compare at least two eligible routes and confirm whether the quote is estimated or final. Decide whether removing retail boxes changes protection or resale needs. Use reinforcement only where the risk justifies its additional weight and volume.

Why review dates matter

This page was reviewed on July 29, 2026. Search snippets and copied articles can preserve older terms after an official service changes. Treat the source date as part of the claim. Reconfirm storage, return eligibility, shipping restrictions and fees inside the service at the moment they affect your order.

Build one deadline sheet for the parcel

A simple private table can prevent expensive mistakes. Use one row per item with the order date, warehouse arrival date, visible after-sales deadline, storage deadline and parcel status. Sort by the earliest deadline. The purpose is not to recreate a public product spreadsheet; it is to keep operational dates attached to the correct item.

Do not fill an unknown deadline with a guessed date. Mark it “confirm” and check the order interface or support response. A blank fact is safer than false precision. If support gives a date, preserve the message or screenshot with the order rather than copying only the number.

Returns can create additional domestic costs

Even when an item is eligible, a return or exchange may involve domestic delivery, seller deductions or time for the replacement to travel back to the warehouse. Decide whether the visible problem justifies the cost and delay. For a measurement mismatch or major wrong variant, the answer may be clear. For a tiny issue, the trade-off depends on your tolerance and the evidence.

Exchanges take longer than simple cancellation because the original item must move back through the seller’s process and the replacement must arrive and be inspected again. Do not submit an international parcel containing the item while that after-sales process is unresolved.

Storage is not a reason to delay inspection

The value of a long storage window is that buyers can consolidate sensible parcels over time. It is not a reason to postpone QC review. Inspection should happen near arrival, when the listing, order details and seller-return state are easiest to compare. Consolidation planning can happen after each item has passed that first decision.

Review older stored items again before parcel submission. Confirm that the warehouse record still shows the expected quantity and that special packaging requests apply to the correct items. If a route restricts a category, separate that item rather than allowing it to remove better shipping options for the rest of the haul.

Questions to answer before parcel submission

  • Has every item passed its visible QC review?
  • Are any return, exchange or support requests still open?
  • Which item has the earliest storage deadline?
  • Do restricted goods reduce the available shipping lines?
  • Is volumetric weight likely to exceed actual weight?
  • Does removing packaging create an unacceptable damage risk?
  • Is the address complete and written in the required format?

Answering these questions will not remove customs or transit risk, but it separates avoidable warehouse decisions from events that occur after dispatch.

Estimate shipping with both weight systems

International lines may charge by actual scale weight or by volumetric weight calculated from parcel dimensions. Before consolidation, note which items are bulky relative to their mass. Shoes with retail boxes, puffer jackets and protective packaging can increase the bill more than their scale weight suggests. A shipping estimate is meaningful only when the packaging assumptions are similar to the final parcel.

Compare routes using the same declared contents and parcel dimensions. A cheaper headline rate can become more expensive after a volume divisor, remote-area fee or restricted-item rule is applied. Also check whether insurance, tracking and tax handling are included or separate. Kakobuys.shop does not publish a single shipping price because those inputs change by destination and route.

Keep customs and legality separate from warehouse approval

Warehouse acceptance or a successful QC review does not mean an item is legal to import into your country. Customs rules, intellectual-property restrictions, batteries, liquids, food, cosmetics and radio devices can create separate requirements. Check destination-country rules and the selected carrier’s prohibited-item list before submission.

Declared value and item descriptions should follow applicable law and the carrier’s instructions. Do not rely on a community spreadsheet to decide customs declarations. The directory is designed for discovery; regulatory responsibility remains with the buyer and the services used for the shipment.

If an item needs special documentation, confirm that requirement before it enters a mixed parcel. Separating the item may preserve more route choices for the remaining goods. Keep the current carrier rule with the parcel record because allowed categories and destination coverage can change between the warehouse arrival date and submission date.
