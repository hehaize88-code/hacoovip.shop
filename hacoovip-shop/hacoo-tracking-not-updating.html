Hacoo Tracking Not Updating: When to Wait and When to Contact Support

A silent tracking page does not automatically mean a lost parcel. Find the last verifiable event, identify the responsible carrier and act on evidence instead of the progress bar.

Quick answer: if Hacoo tracking is not updating, record the last scan’s date, location and carrier; check the same number on the named carrier’s website; look for a local tracking number; and compare the silence with the delivery range shown on your order. Escalate when the estimate has passed or a carrier confirms a concrete exception.

Tracking is a chain of messages created by different logistics systems. Hacoo can display a status supplied by an origin partner, while a destination carrier may not show anything until it receives or scans the parcel. A gap can therefore represent movement without a public event, a handoff between systems, customs processing, missed scanning or a genuine delay. The gap alone cannot tell you which explanation is correct.

Hacoo’s official EU help page currently breaks delivery into three broad periods: preparing the order, shipping it and final delivery, with a published total of 15–28 days. Its separate public shipping page also states that timing varies by destination and that specific dates are not guaranteed. Treat those numbers as planning guidance. Your order’s current estimate and actual dated events are the evidence for your parcel.

Start with the last real tracking event

Ignore the percentage for a moment. Open the full history and write down four details: the exact event wording, timestamp, location and carrier. “59%” or “in transit” is too broad to diagnose. “Information received,” “departed origin facility,” “arrived at destination airport” or “out for delivery” points to a different holder and next action.

Why the Hacoo app and carrier page can disagree

The app may receive summarized events from an aggregator rather than every raw carrier scan. Updates can be translated, grouped or posted after the physical event. The final-mile carrier may also create a second number. That means the Hacoo page can appear behind the carrier page—or occasionally ahead of it—without either record proving that the parcel is lost.

Use both pages, but preserve screenshots with timestamps. If one says “shipped” and the other says “information received,” ask whether the parcel was physically accepted. If the local carrier shows a delivery exception that the app does not, use the carrier wording and reference in your support request. Do not change the tracking number by guessing missing digits or searching unrelated parcel sites that ask for payment or account details.

A seven-step Hacoo tracking audit

  1. Confirm the order. Match the order number, product lines and destination so you do not follow an old or split parcel.
  2. Copy the number. Use copy-and-paste and remove accidental spaces. Record any separate local tracking reference.
  3. Name the carrier. Use the carrier listed on the order or tracking page, not the carrier another shopper received.
  4. Find the newest event. Save the exact wording, time and location rather than the progress percentage.
  5. Compare both systems. Check Hacoo and the carrier’s own public tracking page on the same day.
  6. Check the live range. Note the current estimated final date shown for your order and whether it has passed.
  7. Choose the responsible contact. Ask the carrier about its own confirmed scan; ask Hacoo about pre-handoff, missing reference or after-sales action.

When waiting is reasonable

A short quiet period can be consistent with an international handoff, especially after an origin departure and before the first destination scan. Waiting is also reasonable when the order remains inside its displayed delivery range and neither system shows an exception. Hacoo’s public information warns that regional logistics, natural events and other abnormal conditions can extend timing.

Waiting should still be documented. Save one screenshot now and another later so you can show that nothing changed. Avoid contacting several parties with different descriptions at the same time. A clean chronology is easier to investigate than multiple conflicting claims.

When to contact the carrier

Contact the carrier when its own system shows physical possession: an acceptance scan, destination-depot arrival, delivery attempt, address exception or delivered event. Ask a narrow question such as “What was the location of the final scan?” or “Does this parcel have an open investigation?” Request a case number or written confirmation when available.

The carrier cannot usually explain Hacoo’s internal preparation or refund status. Likewise, Hacoo support may need the carrier to confirm a last-mile loss or misdelivery. Send each party the evidence connected to its stage rather than a complete unrelated order history.

When to contact Hacoo

Contact Hacoo when no carrier has recorded physical acceptance, the order’s delivery range has passed, the order page lacks a usable tracking number, a split shipment is unclear, or the carrier has issued a trace or loss confirmation. Use the order’s support or after-sales route and follow the current prompts. Hacoo’s official delivered-but-missing page specifically directs users to the order page and Refund/Exchange > “I want to refund (Package not received)” when the parcel cannot be found after local checks.

Attach a compact evidence pack

  • Order number and order date
  • Expected delivery range shown in the order
  • Tracking number and named carrier
  • Newest Hacoo event and newest carrier event
  • Screenshot dates and any carrier case number
  • The exact result requested: locate, investigate, correct or refund

How to write a useful tracking request

Keep the first message chronological. For example: “Order ending 7316 was shown as shipped on August 16. Tracking number XX123 last updated at the origin facility on August 19. The named carrier shows the same event, and the order’s latest delivery date was September 1. Please confirm the next carrier or open a parcel investigation.”

This is stronger than “Where is my order?” because it identifies the parcel, the stalled event and the requested action. If support replies with a new estimate, save it. If it supplies a local number, add that to the same record rather than starting a separate timeline.

Do not confuse a tracking delay with a refund delay

A tracking investigation and a refund are separate processes. Submitting a non-receipt request does not mean money has already been sent. Record the application date, review result, refund-processing notice and final credit separately. Hacoo’s published return policy currently displays 8–15 working days for a credit-card refund and 5–7 working days for PayPal after processing; see our Hacoo refund pending guide for the full timeline.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my Hacoo tracking number not working?

It may have been created before the first physical carrier scan, belong to an origin partner rather than the final carrier, or contain a copying error. Confirm the number and carrier on the order page before escalating.

Does no update mean the parcel is lost?

No. A quiet tracking record can occur between logistics systems or scans. A confirmed carrier exception, expired estimate or investigation result provides stronger evidence of a problem.

Should I use a universal tracking website?

Use the carrier named by Hacoo whenever possible. Do not enter payment details or account credentials into an unfamiliar tracking site.

What if the estimated delivery date has passed?

Save the displayed estimate and both tracking records, then contact the party responsible for the last confirmed stage. Ask for a trace or case-specific next step.

Bottom line

When Hacoo tracking is not updating, the last dated scan matters more than the progress bar. Identify the last holder, compare Hacoo with the named carrier, preserve the delivery estimate and escalate only with a specific exception or expired range. That produces a clearer answer and a stronger non-receipt record if the parcel never arrives.
