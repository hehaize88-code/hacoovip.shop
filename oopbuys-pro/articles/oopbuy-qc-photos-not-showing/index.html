Warehouse photos

Oopbuy QC Photos Not Showing After Warehouse Delivery?

Trace a missing Oopbuy QC photo from the seller’s tracking number to the warehouse item, check split deliveries and ask support for the record you need.

Published 3 November 2026

Your parcel says “delivered,” but the hoodie you bought still has no warehouse photos. Open the order line for that hoodie and compare its domestic tracking number with the delivery scan. Then look for a warehouse entry for the same item, colour and size. These checks answer two different questions. The carrier can tell you where it left a package. The item record tells you whether Oopbuy has associated the contents with your order and made photos available. The last event you can actually verify determines what to do next.

Start with the parcel that was delivered

Read the tracking history from the first event. Where did the parcel start, and where was it going? A shipment from a Chinese seller to the warehouse is the domestic leg. A parcel that Oopbuy sent from the warehouse to your home is an international shipment. The word “delivered” can appear on either journey. To find missing warehouse photos, use the number for the seller’s shipment. The number from another parcel you shipped last month will lead to a perfectly valid delivery record for the wrong goods.

Copy the carrier name, shipment number, date and time exactly as displayed. If you also have a screenshot, keep the destination area and final event visible. Check the event itself. A label being created, a parcel arriving at a local depot and a courier recording delivery describe different things. If the last event still shows movement towards the warehouse, there is no recorded handover yet. Go back to the seller’s domestic shipment for the next update.

Match the delivery to one order line

Open the order under the account you used to buy the item. Write down the item reference, seller, selected option and quantity. If you bought two colours from one listing, each colour is a separate check. A photo of a black medium hoodie does little to establish what happened to a grey large one. Even a correct product title may describe both. Match the option shown on the order and, where available, the domestic tracking number.

Check whether the seller supplied more than one tracking number. One payment can cover several pieces and a seller can dispatch them separately. Conversely, several items can travel together in one box. Write one line in your notes for each item and place its known shipment number alongside it. Leave the number blank when you cannot match it. That blank is a useful question for the seller or agent. Guessing which parcel contained the missing item can send support looking for the wrong delivery.

Find the warehouse entry for that item

Oopbuy’s public buying flow places QC photos after goods reach its warehouse. In your account, open the matching order or warehouse item and read its latest recorded stage. Menu wording can vary. Look for the item reference, the selected variant, any issue message and the section containing inspection images. Record exactly what you see. A warehouse item with an empty photo area needs a different follow-up from a shipment with no corresponding warehouse entry at all.

A seller photo, a spreadsheet thumbnail or a public gallery can help identify the design you meant to buy. None links a particular piece to your order. Go back to the signed-in item record before treating any picture as evidence about your parcel. If a thumbnail is present but opening it fails, try the same item again from the order list. Note whether one image fails or every image on that line fails. That detail lets support distinguish a display issue from an absent photo set.

Choose your next step from the last confirmed event

If the carrier shows delivery but you cannot find a warehouse entry, compare the domestic shipment details with the order line once more. Ask whether that shipment has been matched to that order. Include any note about a changed shipment number. The delivery time gives support a point to search from. It does not establish the moment the contents were inspected. There is no need to buy extra photos just to find out whether the correct package was received.

If the warehouse already lists the exact item but no photos are visible, give support the warehouse item reference and ask where its inspection images are or whether inspection is pending. If there is an exception or action request, read it first and mention it in your message. Where your account shows a deadline for a return or a required response, include it. Use the deadline shown for your case and contact support promptly rather than relying on a timetable in a forum post.

If you sent the goods yourself, check the forwarding entry

There is a separate route for an item you bought yourself and asked a seller or another sender to forward to Oopbuy. Oopbuy’s forwarding instructions tell customers to obtain the receiving details, submit the shipment information and identify the goods they are sending. Open your forwarding entry and check the carrier number against the sender’s receipt. A typo, a number from a cancelled label or the tracking number for the other box is enough to make two records look unrelated.

Ask the sender which items they actually packed together. If two boxes went out, obtain both tracking numbers and list the contents they correspond to. Report a missing piece with the contents and reference for the box concerned. This check is specific to self-arranged forwarding. If Oopbuy purchased on your behalf, start with the purchasing order in your account. Creating another forwarding entry would add another reference to a problem that first needs a match.

A worked example with two hoodie colours

Imagine you ordered the same hoodie in black and grey. The seller supplied two domestic tracking numbers. The photo gallery on the black order line shows a garment and a size tag. The grey order line has no photos. You find a delivery scan and are about to ask for a photo of the grey hoodie. First compare the tracking numbers. If the delivered number belongs to the black item and the grey shipment is still in transit, there is no missing warehouse photo to chase yet.

Change one fact. The number that reached the warehouse is the one for the grey hoodie, and the grey item now has its own warehouse reference. The useful question becomes whether inspection images have been attached to that reference. If the account displays photos of a black hoodie under it, save the screenshots with the order options visible and ask staff to verify the association. The same blank gallery led to two different actions once the shipment and the item were identified.

Send one support message that can be answered

A message you can adapt is “My grey hoodie, size L, is order item [reference]. Domestic shipment [number] with [carrier] shows delivery on [date and time]. My warehouse view shows [item reference and status, or no entry]. Can you check whether this shipment was matched to this item and whether its QC photos are available?” If there is already an open conversation, reply there with the new evidence so the person looking at the case can follow the sequence.

Attach the relevant order line and the matching delivery event through the platform’s own support channel. Keep unrelated purchases out of the screenshots. A public post does not need your address, account details or tracking number. Save the reply and note what support checked. If they confirmed receipt but could not yet locate the gallery, you can follow up about that remaining step without asking them to trace the entire parcel again.

When the photos appear, decide what you still need to see

Check the order reference before reviewing the images. Then compare the number of pieces, the chosen colour, the tag and any detail that distinguishes the selected version. For a hoodie, the sewn-in size label answers which size was marked. It does not tell you the garment’s chest width. If fit is the unresolved question, request a clear flat measurement from underarm seam to underarm seam with both ends of the tape visible. For a colour question, ask for a clearer view of the specific panel instead.

Check any charge or conditions displayed for an additional picture before confirming it. If the images already show a clear mismatch, keep the order option and the relevant photo together and ask about the available after-sales steps before submitting the item for international shipping. Your decision can be simple. Approve the item when the photos answer the question that matters, ask for a specific missing detail, or raise a documented mismatch while the item is still at the warehouse.
