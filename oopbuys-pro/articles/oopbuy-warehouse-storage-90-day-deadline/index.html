Warehouse timeline planning

OOPBUY warehouse storage: plan the 90-day deadline.

The useful date is each item's warehouse storage start—not the day you first found it, paid for it or hoped it would arrive.

Checked 5 September 2026Independent warehouse guide

OOPBUY warehouse storage gives separate purchases time to arrive, pass quality control and become one parcel. It is not an unlimited holding area. OOPBuy's current Storage Period page states that items receive 90 days of free storage and shows a paid extension of 10 CNY for 30 days. Platform Protection also advertises 90 days of free storage. Because service terms can change, treat the live item record as the final authority and build your own deadline ledger before the oldest item becomes urgent.

01 · Identify the clock

Start with the warehouse storage date for each item

An order has several dates: payment, seller dispatch, domestic delivery, warehouse receipt, inspection and storage. They are not interchangeable. The OOPBUY warehouse clock should be read from the stored-item record shown in the current account. If the interface displays an expiry date, preserve that date. If it only displays days remaining, record the observation date and update it regularly.

Do not start a single 90-day countdown from the first order in a haul. A sweatshirt received on Monday and a pair of shoes received three weeks later can have different deadlines. Returns, exchanges or a replacement shipment can also change which physical unit is stored and when its relevant record began. The conservative method is one row per warehouse item, not one date for the whole shopping list.

The 90-day figure is a planning window, not a recommendation to wait 89 days. Parcel packing, route selection, payment and operational review all require time. Leave a buffer between your planned submission date and the oldest visible deadline. A deadline ledger is useful precisely because it prevents a cheap item from forcing an expensive, rushed parcel.

02 · Build one source of truth

Create an OOPBUY warehouse ledger that survives a long haul

Record the order number, item description, selected variant, quantity, seller, warehouse receipt status, QC status, storage start or expiry date, return deadline, special attribute, estimated weight and intended parcel group. Add a link or account reference that lets you reopen the exact item. A screenshot is useful evidence, but the live order remains the place to check current actions.

Sort the ledger by the earliest storage expiry, not by price or purchase date. Add three planning fields: target parcel date, buffer days and next action. “Waiting” is not a next action. Use specific labels such as waiting for seller dispatch, request measurement, review QC, decide return, verify restricted-goods route or submit parcel.

Review the ledger whenever a seller ships, a warehouse status changes, new QC photos appear or an after-sales request is opened. During a quiet period, review it at least weekly. As the earliest expiry approaches, shorten that interval. Calendar reminders should point back to the live warehouse record; they should not replace it.

03 · Separate storage from after-sales

A warehouse item can have more than one deadline

Free storage does not mean the seller's return or exchange window remains open for the same period. QC questions should be handled when the evidence appears, not near the storage deadline. Check identity, colour, size label, quantity, visible condition and any measurement you requested while a corrective action may still be possible. The warehouse QC guide gives a repeatable review order.

Keep four clocks separate: seller after-sales, platform processing, free storage and parcel preparation. An item could have plenty of storage days left but very little time for a return. Conversely, an after-sales case may be resolved while the replacement still needs domestic delivery to the warehouse. Your ledger should show both the commercial decision deadline and the storage deadline.

If an item is wrong or damaged, do not submit it merely to simplify the storage plan. Preserve the order status, QC evidence and conversation record, then use the live after-sales path available for that order. If it is correct, mark the approval clearly so it does not require another full review when parcel submission becomes urgent.

04 · Decide whether to wait

Compare the value of consolidation with the cost of delay

Waiting can be rational when several confirmed items will soon be ready and one consolidated parcel is compatible with their routes. It becomes risky when a seller has not dispatched, an exchange is uncertain, the oldest item is close to expiry or the combined package may become too large for a preferred line.

Model at least two plans. Plan A ships ready items by a safe date. Plan B waits for the late item and shows the later submission date, possible extension charge, estimated parcel size and route effect. Compare total cost and retained risk, not just the number of cartons. The combine-or-split guide helps test those parcel scenarios.

A small late item can still be expensive if waiting causes an extension, removes a route, delays a seasonal need or forces the oldest goods into a rushed decision. A bulky arrival can change volumetric weight. A restricted item can narrow routes for an otherwise ordinary clothing parcel. Set a latest acceptable arrival date for every unfinished order, then split the parcel if the evidence no longer supports waiting.

05 · Use extensions deliberately

Verify the current storage-extension offer before relying on it

OOPBuy's public Storage Period page checked on 5 September 2026 shows 10 CNY for a 30-day extension after the 90-day free period. That published snapshot is useful evidence, but it should not be treated as a permanent universal price or proof that every item can always be extended. Confirm the live amount, eligible item, purchased period, new deadline and successful payment in the current account.

An extension buys time; it does not repair a missed return window, guarantee route availability or protect an item from policy changes. Before paying, write down the specific event you are waiting for and the date on which waiting stops. If there is no defined event, the extension may simply postpone the same decision.

Compare the extension with shipping now, splitting the parcel, returning an unresolved item when eligible, or abandoning a low-value item only if the platform's current process permits it. Include every related cost. Paying a modest extension can make sense for a valuable, compatible haul, while repeated extensions can erase the saving that motivated the order.

06 · Protect the last mile

Leave enough time for parcel submission and payment

Choose an internal parcel deadline earlier than the earliest warehouse expiry. Use that buffer to confirm included items, destination, declaration, packing options, measured or estimated dimensions, eligible routes, current freight quote and payment. If the packed parcel differs materially from the estimate, you need time to understand the difference rather than approve it under pressure.

Before submission, open every included warehouse record and reconcile it with the parcel list. Check that returns or exchanges are closed, quantities are correct and no late item was accidentally omitted or included. Then save the parcel reference and the terms that matter for the selected route. Storage planning ends only when the warehouse items have successfully moved into the submitted parcel workflow.

Warehouse deadline checklist

  • Record one storage date or expiry per item.
  • Sort by the earliest deadline.
  • Keep after-sales deadlines separate.
  • Give every waiting item a next action.
  • Set a safe internal parcel date.
  • Compare ship-now and wait scenarios.
  • Verify extension eligibility and price live.
  • Confirm the new deadline after payment.
  • Recheck QC and parcel contents.
  • Preserve the final parcel reference.
