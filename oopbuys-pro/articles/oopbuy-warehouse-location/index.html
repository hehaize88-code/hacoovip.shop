Warehouse location guide

OOPBUY warehouse location: verify the address and arrival.

Verify the receiving instructions, match the seller shipment and know what warehouse arrival actually proves.

Published October 5, 2026Independent editorial guideApprox. 8 minute read

OOPBUY's published beginner guide describes a warehouse in China, where purchased items are received, inspected and stored before international parcel submission. That establishes the country and the role of the warehouse. It does not establish a permanent street address for every order. If you need the OOPBUY warehouse location for a delivery, use receiving instructions confirmed for your current order. This guide explains how to connect those instructions with the seller shipment, warehouse record and QC photographs, so an address search turns into a useful receiving check.

OOPBUY warehouse location: the information you can verify

The official beginner guide identifies the receiving stage as the OOPBUY China warehouse. A city copied from a forum, an address on an old shipping label, and the receiving destination confirmed for your parcel are different kinds of evidence. Only the last one can reliably guide a new delivery. We have not verified a permanent public street address suitable for publication here, and we do not present a guessed city or postal code as an official instruction.

Start by identifying why you need the location. If you placed a purchasing-agent order, the practical question is whether the seller shipment belongs to that order and has reached the correct warehouse record. If you intend to send goods independently, first confirm that OOPBUY supports the exact service you want to use and obtain its current instructions. An ordinary shopping guide is not authorization to send an unsolicited parcel to a facility.

A delivery address also needs its supporting fields. The recipient name, telephone field, account reference, carrier requirements and any identifying remark can matter as much as the street. Copy the instructions together and preserve the original writing when the carrier needs it. Do not translate a Chinese address yourself and then discard the original. Ask support to clarify any field that you cannot match to the shipping form.

Build a receiving record before the seller dispatches

Keep a small record for each order rather than one note for your whole haul. Save the OOPBUY order reference, the source listing, selected size and color, quantity, seller name, domestic tracking number when available, and the receiving instructions associated with the shipment. These are your own organizational checks, not additional published OOPBUY requirements. Their purpose is to let you explain a mismatch without reconstructing several purchases from memory.

For a manually arranged shipment, obtain confirmation before dispatch. State what you intend to send, which order or service it belongs to, and which receiving information you have been given. Avoid sending a package merely because a seller says an old address is familiar. A successful delivery for another account does not prove that your shipment will be recognized or handled under the same arrangement.

  • Match the receiving instructions to the current order or supported service.
  • Keep the complete address and any required identifying reference together.
  • Record the seller's actual dispatch confirmation separately from an estimated date.
  • Save the domestic carrier name and tracking number without substituting the international parcel number.
  • Check whether multiple purchased items are being sent in one seller shipment or separate shipments.

If the order contains two sizes of the same product, use separate lines in your record. A tracking number alone cannot show whether both variants were enclosed. The matching task is easier when the expected quantity and option are already written down. Product photographs and order screenshots should support the record, while the live order remains the place to check its latest state.

Seller delivery, warehouse check-in and QC are separate checkpoints

A domestic carrier's delivered event answers a transport question: the carrier recorded delivery of that shipment. It does not, by itself, prove which items were inside, whether each item has been matched to your account, or whether photographs are ready. Treat carrier delivery, the stored-item record and visible QC evidence as separate checkpoints. Collapsing them into one event makes ordinary uncertainty harder to explain.

After delivery is recorded, look for the corresponding item in your current warehouse view. Compare its order reference, variant and quantity with your receiving record. Then inspect the photographs that are actually available. The OOPBUY QC guide covers identity, measurements and visible condition; it should follow the receiving check rather than replace it. A clear image of an item is not enough if it belongs to a different order.

Do not create an invented processing deadline. The sources reviewed for this guide do not establish a universal number of hours between a carrier delivery scan and finished warehouse photographs. A current service notice or a reply about your specific order is more useful than a fixed promise copied from another buyer. Preserve timestamps and compare the exact stage when asking for an update.

When the record is missing or inconsistent, send one concise inquiry containing the order reference, domestic carrier, tracking number, last scan and expected contents. State what is absent: the entire warehouse item, one of several pieces, the selected variant, or the QC photographs. Ask for the next action and the relevant current processing estimate. Keep the reply with the same order record so later follow-up adds evidence instead of restarting the explanation.

A worked receiving example with two seller shipments

Consider an illustrative purchase of a sweatshirt and a pair of shoes. The seller sends the sweatshirt under domestic tracking reference A and the shoes under reference B. Reference A records delivery on Monday. Reference B is still in transit. On Tuesday the account shows the sweatshirt stored, but the shoes are not visible. This example is an organizational exercise, not a claim about OOPBUY's typical processing time.

The correct comparison is item by item. Match the sweatshirt's warehouse record to reference A and review its QC. Continue following reference B for the shoes. Reporting that the entire order is missing because one parcel is still travelling would obscure the useful detail. Equally, assuming that both items arrived because one tracking number says delivered would skip the second shipment completely.

Now change the facts: both references show delivery, but the shoe record displays a different size. Save the selected size from the order and the label visible in the QC image. Ask OOPBUY to investigate that precise discrepancy through the live order's after-sales controls. The destination address is no longer the main issue; item identification is. This is why the address, shipment and product records belong in one chain of evidence.

Keep warehouse receiving details separate from return instructions

A warehouse location is not automatically a return destination. A return can involve the marketplace seller, an approved after-sales case and instructions specific to that case. Before arranging a return, obtain the applicable destination, reference, packaging requirements and deadline through the current order process. Do not reuse a receiving address merely because it appeared on the original shipment.

Also distinguish returning an item within China from returning something after international delivery. The available process and costs need to be checked for the actual situation. The OOPBUY return and refund guide helps separate those stages. This page does not publish a universal return address or imply that every stored item is still eligible for return.

Move from a verified warehouse item to a planned parcel

Once the correct item is stored, record its displayed storage information and any separate after-sales deadline. Do this individually for each item; a later purchase does not reset an older item's clock. Use the warehouse storage planner to set a practical review date before you become dependent on an urgent parcel submission.

Next, group only items that are ready for the same shipment. Check QC, destination, current route eligibility and packaging needs before comparing quotes. Your warehouse record establishes what is available to ship; the shipping cost guide explains how the packed parcel changes the estimate. Knowing the warehouse country does not reveal the final freight charge or guarantee a delivery date.

Before closing the receiving task, make sure every expected item has a clear state: still with the seller, travelling domestically, delivered but awaiting clarification, stored and under review, or ready for a parcel. That short list makes the next decision visible. It is also more useful to support than a collection of screenshots without order references or a generic question about where the warehouse is located.
