Product links

Why Won’t My Taobao or Weidian Link Open on Oopbuy?

Check a broken product link, identify the right listing and size, and understand Oopbuy’s item-unavailable message before you order.

Published 3 November 2026

Start with the link at the seller’s end. Open it outside your spreadsheet and check whether it takes you to one item with a shop name and selectable options. If it does, copy that product’s address into Oopbuy’s product search. If it takes you to a shop, a login screen or an unavailable item, you have learned something different in each case. Finding out where the lookup stops saves more time than trying different links at random.

What did you actually copy?

A spreadsheet cell can show a product name while linking to a shop, a seller’s album, a social post or a buying agent. Open the cell and look at the page you land on. Can you identify the seller and the item itself? Could you choose the size, colour or pack you want from this screen? A photograph with a caption can help you recognise a jacket, but it cannot tell Oopbuy which seller listing to retrieve.

A share from a shopping app may contain a sentence, a short address, an invitation to open the app and a product name, all in the same copied text. Open just the address using the original marketplace. If the page appears, copy its product link afresh. Keep the original share until you have checked the new link. A short link that resolves correctly on your phone can still be a poor reference for someone who opens your spreadsheet on a computer.

Identify the marketplace and the item number

Look for the marketplace name together with the number that identifies the whole listing. A familiar Taobao pattern is item.taobao.com/item.htm?id=ITEM_ID. A familiar Weidian pattern is weidian.com/item.html?itemID=ITEM_ID. ITEM_ID is a placeholder in these examples. The addresses illustrate what to look for when reading a link you already have. Mobile and shared addresses can look different and still lead to the same item.

Keep the marketplace name with its number. A string of digits copied from a seller’s profile, an order confirmation or a colour option might identify an entirely different thing. If a URL has both a product identifier and a variant identifier, preserve the original full address and record your chosen option separately. Deleting everything after the question mark would also delete the product number in both examples above. Use the marketplace’s own copy-link action if the address is hard to interpret.

Test the seller page and the Oopbuy page separately

First check the seller listing. If you see a login page or an app prompt, use the marketplace’s normal access process if you already have access. Until you can see the item, its availability is unknown. If you see a specific seller notice saying the listing has been removed, record that result. For a saved find, ask the person who shared it for a current listing from the same seller. An old thumbnail alone gives you little basis for choosing a replacement.

Next paste the product link into the field for a product name or link on Oopbuy. Look at the result before you add anything to your cart. Oopbuy’s public product interface lists an unavailable item, an expired link and a closed store among possible explanations for a failed lookup. The message does not identify which one occurred. If the original seller page opens and the same link fails in Oopbuy, keep those two observations together when asking for help.

A product that opens can still be the wrong choice

Compare the seller, item number, model and options across the two pages. Pay attention to what the price buys. A headline price can belong to an accessory, a single piece of a set or a different size. Select the intended combination and read the amount beside that selection before relying on the price in your saved list. An unavailable size on an otherwise working product page is a stock question about that option. Another copy of the link will not make the size available.

Consider an illustrative example. A spreadsheet describes a navy overshirt in size M. The seller page opens with several colours, and the Oopbuy page opens with the first colour selected. The first photograph still shows navy. Compare the option label, the selected size and the quantity rather than trusting that first photograph. If you cannot find the same combination on both pages, stop before payment. A seller can clarify whether two labels describe the same colour. A different customer’s warehouse photo cannot confirm which option is in your cart.

When a saved listing disappears or leads somewhere new

A seller may give you a new product address after an old listing disappears. Treat the new item number as a fresh comparison. Check who sells it, what is included, how the sizing is described and whether the same options can be selected. If the photos have been reused on multiple listings, compare details such as the closure, the number of pieces and the size chart. Write down any difference that would change your purchase. A matching cover picture is a reason to look closer, not a confirmation that the two offers are equivalent.

If you have already paid for an order, open the existing order record before considering another listing. Save the order reference, the selected variant and the latest status. Contact Oopbuy about that order if the public link has changed. A seller changing a listing does not by itself tell you whether the original item was purchased, cancelled or sent to the warehouse. Ordering a replacement before checking the first order can leave you with two separate purchases.

What to do with a purchase restriction

Read the wording on the Oopbuy screen. The public interface includes a warning for products that Oopbuy says it cannot purchase and a separate purchase notice. A specific refusal calls for a different next step from a link that simply fails to load. Follow the notice shown for the actual item. If you need help interpreting it, use the official support channel and include the product reference. Changing platforms, rewriting the product name or copying another seller’s image does not resolve the restriction.

A support request someone can reproduce

Include the marketplace, the original product address, the item number if visible, the option you want and the exact message you saw. Specify whether the original listing opened, whether you reached Oopbuy’s product page and at which step the failure occurred. If a particular size disappeared only after you selected a colour, say so. A cropped screenshot showing the selected option and the message is more informative than a full-screen image of an empty cart. Keep your home address, payment details and unrelated orders out of the screenshot.

For example, a useful support question would be “The seller page opens for this Weidian item. I can select navy and size M there. On Oopbuy I can open the same item but do not see size M under navy. Could you confirm whether that combination can be ordered?” If the page did not open at all, describe exactly that instead. The recipient can now investigate one identifiable listing and one observable problem.

Update the spreadsheet row after the check

Keep one line with the source marketplace, item number, working product link, selected option and date checked. Leave a brief note such as “seller page opens; size M unavailable” or “new seller link supplied; size chart differs”. If you keep both the new and old references, label them so that another reader does not accidentally choose the obsolete one. The next person opening the sheet can repeat your check even if the title has been translated differently.

Before you order, reopen the chosen variant and verify the quantity and price once more. After an item reaches the warehouse, the QC photos help you check whether the piece received matches what you selected. Saving the listing and option now gives you something precise to compare against those photos later. If the unresolved problem is that the photos have not appeared, switch to the warehouse-arrival guide rather than starting the product search over.
