How to Use an ACBuy Spreadsheet to Check Product Links and Finds

Use a six-field record to compare ACBuy finds, check prices and variants on the seller’s page, and decide what to verify in warehouse QC photos.

Updated 2 November 2026

Take one row all the way to a buying decision

Pick a candidate in the finds catalogue. Write down the marketplace, listing ID and seller before comparing its price with a second row. Now choose the exact variant on the live listing. If the low price belongs to a different size or to an accessory, replace the spreadsheet price in your notes with the amount for the item you would actually order.

An ACBuy spreadsheet can turn an endless product search into a manageable shortlist. It may group shoes, clothing, bags or accessories and place useful reference fields beside each item. That convenience is real, but it creates a common mistake: treating the row as if it were the product. It is not. A spreadsheet is a saved description of what an editor saw at one moment; the live seller listing is where the order choices exist.

The useful question is therefore not “Is this spreadsheet good?” but “Can I still verify this specific candidate?” A reliable workflow checks the link identity, seller, variant, quantity, price context and shipping implications before money changes hands. This guide gives Romania and international buyers a repeatable method that works even when a row is old, a seller edits the listing, or an imported product page needs manual details.

What an ACBuy spreadsheet can—and cannot—do

A well-maintained sheet is valuable for discovery. It can standardize category labels, make comparable items easier to scan and preserve a reference when a listing title is difficult to search. It can also record the editor’s last review date. Those functions reduce research time.

It cannot guarantee stock, current price, authenticity, international eligibility or future product quality. It cannot show which variant a low headline price represents unless the editor recorded that detail. Nor can an old warehouse image prove that the next unit from the seller will be identical. Use each row as a lead that must pass a fresh check.

Step 1: confirm the link’s identity before judging the item

Open the candidate and look at the final page, not only the anchor text. Redirects happen, listings disappear, and a seller may reuse a page for a changed product. Confirm that the page still describes the same category and general model as the spreadsheet row. A title mismatch, different main image or unrelated options is a reason to pause.

Record the source marketplace and a stable listing identifier if one is visible in the URL. Then note the seller name shown today. This creates a compact identity record: marketplace, listing ID, seller and review date. If the page later changes, you can distinguish a normal variant update from a completely different listing.

Fast rejection rule: reject a row when the destination is dead, the product category has changed, the seller cannot be identified, or the required option no longer exists. A similar thumbnail is not enough.

Step 2: decode the headline price

The smallest visible number may belong to an accessory, deposit, single component or cheapest size. Open the variant selector and choose the exact combination you want before recording a working price. If the listing uses quantity tiers, identify the quantity needed to reach a displayed level. Do not multiply a promotional minimum by your quantity unless that price actually applies.

Keep the domestic product amount separate from seller-to-warehouse delivery. International shipping is a later parcel decision, so a spreadsheet should never present the item price as a delivered total. Add columns for selected variant price, domestic freight shown at order review, and “not yet known” international delivery. This prevents a cheap row from looking artificially complete.

Step 3: turn variant names into an order specification

Variant accuracy matters more than a polished product title. Write the selected colour, size, version and quantity exactly as the live page presents them. For clothing and shoes, save the seller’s measurements and compare them with an item you already own. A letter size alone is weak evidence because sizing conventions vary between sellers.

If automatic import does not show the expected options, do not improvise. Re-open the original listing and check whether the URL contains tracking parameters or a mobile wrapper that can be removed. When a manual order form is presented, supply a concise specification and use remarks only for details that cannot be selected directly. “Black, size 42, one pair” is actionable; “same as picture, best quality” is not.

Step 4: evaluate seller and listing signals without inventing certainty

Seller activity, dispatch history and listing detail can help prioritize candidates, but no single signal proves the next item’s condition. Prefer a page with unambiguous variants, consistent product images and enough information to identify what will be purchased. Be more cautious when the title, photos and options appear to describe different products.

Recent sales or feedback can be context where visible, but screenshots copied into a spreadsheet may be stale. Check the current page and treat translated text carefully. The goal is not to assign a universal seller score. It is to determine whether the purchase request is specific enough that the delivered unit can later be compared against it.

This structure is deliberately small. A spreadsheet with forty decorative columns often hides the facts that drive a purchase. Six consistently maintained fields are more useful than a large sheet full of empty ratings or unverifiable claims.

Step 5: decide what warehouse evidence would change your mind

Before ordering, write down the visible details that would cause you to keep, question or return the item. For shoes, these might include the size label, pair symmetry, outsole, visible marks and included accessories. For a hoodie, use colour, printed or embroidered placement, tagged size and key measurements. This converts quality control from casual photo browsing into a decision.

Warehouse photographs can show visible condition and variant clues, but they do not prove material composition, authenticity or long-term durability. Camera angle and lighting can also distort colour. If a decision-critical feature is not visible, use the order interface’s available clarification or photo options before creating the international parcel. Do not assume that an older image attached to a spreadsheet represents your unit.

Step 6: screen for parcel risk before building a haul

A verified product may still be a poor parcel choice. Consider whether the item is unusually bulky, fragile, liquid-filled, battery-powered or otherwise likely to face route restrictions. Also consider whether protective packaging or keeping a retail box matters to you. These choices can change dimensions and billable weight later.

For Romanian buyers, destination rules and available lines must be checked using current parcel data. Do not use an old sheet’s shipping estimate as a promise. Until the item is at the warehouse and the parcel is defined, treat international cost as a planning range rather than a fixed number.

A worked comparison without fake prices

Suppose two rows show visually similar shoes. Candidate A has a live listing, the chosen size is selectable, measurements are clear and domestic delivery appears separately at order review. Candidate B shows a lower headline number, but that number belongs to replacement laces; the desired shoe option is unclear and the seller page has changed since the sheet was reviewed.

Candidate A is the stronger choice even before comparing exact totals. Its order specification is testable. Candidate B forces assumptions about both product and price. This example intentionally avoids invented platform fees: the point is that evidence quality should be compared before cost arithmetic begins.

When to update, quarantine or remove a row

  • Update when the same listing and seller remain available but price, variants or domestic delivery have changed.
  • Quarantine when the page works but identity or options are ambiguous. Keep it out of featured recommendations until checked.
  • Remove when the link is dead, redirects to an unrelated category, or no longer supports the recorded product.
  • Recheck before ordering when the last review date is old, even if the row remains in the sheet.

A review date should describe when a person actually opened and assessed the live listing. It should not update automatically merely because the website was rebuilt. That distinction keeps freshness labels meaningful.

Final pre-order checklist

  1. Open the final destination and confirm the product has not changed.
  2. Record marketplace, listing ID, seller and today’s review date.
  3. Select the exact variant and identify what the displayed price covers.
  4. Separate product price, domestic delivery and unknown international shipping.
  5. Save measurements or specification details needed for warehouse comparison.
  6. Define visible keep, question and return conditions before purchase.
  7. Check product characteristics that could restrict parcel routes.
  8. Use live order and parcel screens as the final authority for current amounts and options.

The best ACBuy spreadsheet is not the one with the most rows. It is the one that makes uncertainty visible and helps you discard weak candidates quickly. Treat discovery, ordering, warehouse review and international shipping as separate decisions. That habit produces a smaller shortlist, clearer purchase instructions and fewer surprises than copying an old link and hoping every field is still true.
