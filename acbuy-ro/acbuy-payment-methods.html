ACBuy Payment Methods: Compare Checkout Totals and Trace a Failed Payment

Identify which charge you are paying, compare the final amount in one currency and match a pending payment to its order before trying again.

Updated 2 November 2026

Match three records before paying again

A paid top-up, an unpaid order and a temporary card authorisation can appear at the same time. Put the amount, currency and timestamp beside the reference for each. If the balance increased but the product order is still unpaid, open that order to see whether a separate balance payment is required. If your provider shows a pending or completed charge and the ACBuy record is unclear, ask the provider or agent to identify the transaction before attempting it again. Share the references through the official account channel and conceal full payment details.

A payment can look unsuccessful in one place and unfinished in another. The checkout may return an error, the banking application may show a transaction, and the order may still say that money is due. Paying again immediately makes that situation harder to understand. A more useful approach is to identify which transaction you attempted, collect its references, and reconcile the records before creating another attempt.

This ACBuy payment methods guide explains that checking process. It does not publish a permanent list of supported cards, wallets or transfer providers. Availability can depend on the account, country, currency and transaction being paid. The options displayed inside the authenticated checkout are the evidence for your order; a payment logo in a third-party article is not confirmation that the method is currently available.

Identify the payment before choosing a method

Write down whether you are paying for merchandise, adding account funds, paying an international parcel charge or responding to an adjustment. These are different records even when they appear in the same account. A successful balance top-up does not, by itself, demonstrate that an individual order was paid. Likewise, an item purchase does not prove that the later international shipment has been funded.

Open the relevant order or parcel and record its reference and amount due. Compare that record with the payment screen before continuing. If an adjustment appears, identify the item or service it belongs to. Do not approve an unexplained difference simply because its amount is small: an incorrect quantity or variant can produce a larger problem after purchasing.

Compare options using the checkout evidence

For every method actually offered, compare the amount charged, billing currency, separately listed fee, expected processing information and refund instructions. Look for an expiry time on any payment request. If the displayed currency differs from your card or bank account currency, check the conversion information provided by both the checkout and your provider. A low service fee alone does not establish the lowest total.

Keep a small transaction record

Save the order reference, payment reference, timestamp with timezone, amount, currency and the exact message shown. A screenshot of the status can help, but it should accompany the reference numbers rather than replace them. Keep the record private. Remove full card numbers, account balances unrelated to the transaction, addresses and authentication codes before sharing evidence with support.

Use a simple naming pattern such as “order reference, first attempt, local time” so two attempts cannot be confused. If you later receive a refund or reversal, add that as a separate entry linked to the original attempt. Changing the original note to “refunded” would hide the sequence that support may need to investigate.

When checkout rejects a payment before completion

Read the actual message. An incorrect billing field, an expired payment request and an authentication problem require different responses. Check that the selected order remains available, the amount has not changed and any provider verification was completed through its normal interface. Correct a clearly identified input problem once rather than repeatedly switching methods without understanding the result.

If the message is unclear, inspect both the order and provider records before trying again. Do not change your country, identity or product description to avoid a restriction. When the checkout explicitly says a method is unavailable, choose another method only if it is offered legitimately and the first attempt has been resolved.

When your provider shows money but the order is unpaid

Record the provider's exact status instead of treating every visible entry as a completed charge. Ask the provider what that status means for this transaction and whether any action is required. Separately ask ACBuy to reconcile the payment reference with the order. Neither a checkout error nor a banking screenshot alone tells you the complete outcome.

Do not invent a universal waiting period from another customer's experience. Follow the processing information supplied for your actual method, and record the date on which support says to follow up. If the provider confirms completion while the platform cannot locate the payment, provide the matching reference, amount and timestamp through authenticated support.

Resolve the first attempt before paying twice

Repeated retries can create several records with the same amount. Before another attempt, establish whether the earlier one failed, remains unresolved or succeeded but has not been allocated correctly. If the order is time-sensitive, ask support to explain the available options and the status of the existing payment rather than assuming that a fresh order is necessary.

When two completed payments appear to relate to one order, report both references in a single case. State which purchase you intended to make and request reconciliation. Do not describe a transaction as unauthorized unless that is accurate. Keep any deadlines communicated by the provider or platform in your notes.

Separate a refund decision from receipt of money

An order cancellation, an approved refund and a returned amount are separate pieces of evidence. Check where the refund is supposed to go and which original payment it relates to. If an account credit appears, confirm whether it is usable balance or whether another withdrawal step is available under the current terms. Do not assume that every refund returns directly to the original method.

Keep the refund reference and the expected processing information supplied for that case. The returns and exchanges guide explains the product-side decision; payment reconciliation begins after you know which refund or adjustment was authorized.

A worked example with two conflicting screens

Imagine a buyer submits one merchandise payment. Checkout shows an error, but the provider lists an entry for the same amount. The buyer records both screens, checks that the order is still unpaid, and asks the provider to explain its entry. They then send ACBuy the order and transaction references together. This is an illustrative checking sequence, not a report about an actual customer.

If the first attempt is confirmed unsuccessful, the buyer can review the current checkout and decide whether to retry. If it is confirmed complete, the next step is allocation or reconciliation. If it remains unresolved, a second attempt would create another uncertainty. The appropriate action follows the evidence, not the colour of a single status badge.

Write a support request that can be investigated

State the transaction type, order or parcel reference, payment reference, amount, currency and time. Quote the exact conflicting statuses, explain whether you retried, and ask one specific question: “Please confirm whether transaction A was received and allocated to order B.” Attach only the relevant redacted evidence. Keep replies in the same case so the history stays together.

Never provide a password, complete card security details or a one-time authentication code in a support message. Enter financial information only through the legitimate payment flow. If someone proposes a new recipient or an off-platform payment, verify it independently through the authenticated account before sending money.

Finish with three matching records

Before moving on, reconcile the provider's outcome, the platform's payment record and the individual order or parcel. Save the final receipt and remove duplicate drafts only after their status is clear. Continue with the ordering workflow for merchandise, or the international parcel tracking guide after a shipment is submitted. A complete record makes later questions about adjustments or refunds much easier to resolve.
