ACBuy Parcel Tracking: From Warehouse Dispatch to Delivery

Track an ACBuy international parcel using the correct references, distinguish label creation from carrier scans, and prepare evidence when updates stop.

Reviewed 4 October 2026 · Independent guide
EnglishRomână

After submitting an international parcel, it is easy to keep checking the wrong number. Your product order, the seller's domestic shipment and the international parcel can each have separate references. A domestic delivery scan at the warehouse does not mean your international shipment has reached your country. Start by identifying the parcel record you are actually trying to follow.

This guide concerns the journey after international parcel submission. For purchasing and movement from the seller to the warehouse, use the ACBuy order status guide. Keeping the two stages separate prevents an old domestic tracking result from being mistaken for a new international update.

Create a parcel reference sheet

Save the international parcel number, selected shipping service, destination, submission date, displayed tracking number and the first available shipment event. Record the included product orders separately. If the platform later supplies a different local tracking reference, add it to the same sheet with the carrier name and the date you received it.

Copy references exactly, including letters and leading zeros. Avoid adding spaces from a screenshot or confusing the parcel ID with a carrier tracking code. When asking support for help, supply both references if they exist and clearly label them. You do not need to share your full address publicly to ask what a status means.

Read what the latest event actually proves

A status may record information being created, a physical handover, movement through a network or a request for action. These are different kinds of evidence. UPS, for example, distinguishes label creation from possession of a shipment in its tracking explanations. That example illustrates a general reading habit; it does not mean an ACBuy parcel uses UPS or that other carriers use identical labels.

Read the event description together with its timestamp and location. A tracking number becoming searchable is not sufficient evidence of a flight departure. Likewise, a destination-country reference may be assigned before the parcel arrives there. Use the carrier's actual explanation rather than inferring movement from a familiar city name.

Use a stage-based checking routine

The same short phrase may have different meanings in different networks. When the carrier supplies an explanation, use that explanation for its service. A third-party tracking application can help organize events, but a translated summary should not override the carrier's underlying record.

Handle a period without new scans

First identify the last meaningful event. “No update since label creation” and “no update after a recorded international handover” describe different situations. Compare the gap with the current guidance for the service you purchased, not an unrelated screenshot from another route. Keep dates and timezones consistent when comparing two tracking systems.

There is no universal number of days after which every quiet tracking page means a parcel is lost. Ask the platform or responsible carrier when an investigation can be opened for your service. Record that answer and any claim deadline. Continue checking for messages requiring action so a document request is not mistaken for ordinary transit silence.

Check the destination carrier when it is identified

Use the local carrier shown in the actual parcel record or supplied by support. Do not assume that a route name tells you which company will complete delivery. If the tracking references change, preserve the relationship between the international and local numbers. Ask support to confirm that relationship when it is not visible.

For eligible postal items handled by Poșta Română, its official Track & Trace guidance describes a 13-character postal reference and notes limits on information during transit through other countries. Other carriers can use different formats. An unrecognized number on the Romanian postal website does not establish a lost parcel if a different company handles the shipment.

Respond carefully to requests for information

A delivery or customs-related message should be checked against the actual parcel and the organization's official channel. Compare the reference and requested action. Open the legitimate account or carrier service independently rather than relying only on an unexpected payment link. If the request is genuine, provide the required accurate documents through that channel and preserve the confirmation.

Keep invoices, order details, payment evidence and the parcel contents list available in case they are requested. Do not alter a document or invent a lower value to make a request easier to complete. Ask the responsible operator to explain any unclear field or charge before submitting inaccurate information.

Prepare one complete investigation request

Include the parcel ID, tracking number, route, destination country, last event and date, and the action you have already taken. Ask a specific question: whether the carrier has received the parcel, which organization owns the next handover, or what evidence is needed to start an investigation. Attach relevant screenshots with private details redacted.

Keep the case number and replies together. Starting several disconnected cases can make the chronology harder to reconstruct. If support provides a follow-up date, record it. If a deadline for a claim or response is approaching, raise that fact explicitly rather than assuming that an open conversation automatically preserves every option.

When tracking says delivered but you cannot find it

Read the carrier's delivery details and check the location, recipient information or collection instructions that are actually provided. Ask household members or an authorized reception point where appropriate. If the evidence does not explain the delivery, contact the carrier and platform promptly with the tracking reference and the specific discrepancy.

Do not accuse an individual based only on a status label. State the facts: the record says delivered at a particular time, but the parcel has not been located after the stated checks. Preserve any delivery photograph or signature information available through the legitimate service. Follow the applicable investigation process.

When the parcel arrives damaged or incomplete

Photograph the exterior, label, seals and visible damage before discarding packaging. Document the contents and compare them with the parcel's included-item list and warehouse evidence. If a quantity is missing, identify the relevant product order and what was expected. Keep packaging and records while the responsible party explains the evidence needed for a claim.

A general unboxing photograph may not establish which item is absent or where the problem occurred. A clear sequence and a matching list are more useful. The QC guide explains how saved warehouse photographs can support comparison, while the applicable carrier and service terms determine the actual investigation.

An example of a useful tracking log

Imagine a buyer records parcel submission on Monday, tracking information creation on Tuesday and a physical carrier event later in the week. The buyer writes each event as observed rather than changing Tuesday's entry to “shipped.” When a local reference appears, they add it alongside the original number. This is an illustrative timeline without promised handling times.

If updates then stop, support receives a concise history with the last confirmed physical event. If a document request appears, the log records when it was received and answered. This makes it possible to distinguish an unresolved handover from an unanswered request without guessing from elapsed days alone.

Close the record after delivery

Keep the shipment and payment records until any delivery discrepancy has been resolved. Record the actual arrival date separately from the estimate, and note the route and parcel characteristics if you want to compare future shipments. One successful delivery is useful personal evidence, but it is not a guarantee that the next parcel will take the same time.

For the next order, improve the inputs you control: complete recipient details, accurate contents, appropriate packaging and saved references. Review the rehearsal packing guide when measurements are uncertain. Good tracking starts with a well-documented parcel and continues by separating confirmed events from assumptions.
