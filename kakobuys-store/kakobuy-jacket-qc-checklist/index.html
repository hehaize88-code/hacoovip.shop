Kakobuy Jacket QC Checklist: Measurements, Zipper and Lining

Check Kakobuy jacket QC photos for chest and sleeve measurements, front alignment, zipper details, lining, pockets and visible construction.

Jacket QC needs more than a neat front view. Layers, closures, lining and shaped sleeves can conceal problems or distort a measurement. This Kakobuy jacket QC checklist helps you review warehouse images in a useful order and request the missing evidence that matters. Adapt it to the actual design: a shell, bomber, overshirt and padded jacket should not be judged against one universal silhouette.

At a glance

Confirm the variant, review front and back, compare relaxed chest, body and sleeve measurements by matching methods, then inspect the zipper or buttons, collar, pockets, cuffs and lining. Separate visible checks from warmth, waterproofing and durability claims that photos cannot establish.

01

Confirm the construction you actually ordered

Open the order beside the gallery and check color, size, closure type, hood option and any removable parts. Similar seller photos may represent lined and unlined versions, different lengths or alternative pocket layouts. Match the ordered option before judging whether a missing hood or different collar is a mistake.

Read the garment label if it is visible, but do not use a familiar size letter as a fit guarantee. Save the seller chart and the option details used when ordering. A jacket can have the correct label yet differ from the dimensions you expected. Conversely, a loose-looking flat photograph does not establish that it will feel roomy over your intended layers.

Review the whole garment in front and rear views before zooming in. Note which areas are hidden by an open front, folded sleeve or hood. The initial goal is an evidence map: what is shown clearly, what appears questionable, and what has not been shown at all.

02

Choose a reference jacket for the same layering purpose

Measure a jacket you already use for a comparable purpose. A close-fitting overshirt is a weak reference for a padded coat worn over a thick sweater. Record the reference garment's closure state, chest width, body-length path and sleeve method. Use those same conditions for the warehouse comparison wherever practical.

Chest width should identify the underarm endpoints and whether the front is closed naturally. Do not stretch the panel to align a tape. Padded garments have thickness and internal space that an external flat width cannot fully describe. Treat the number as a comparison aid, not an exact measure of usable room around the body.

For length, specify high shoulder point to hem or back-neck seam to hem and stay with that method. A curved or dropped rear hem may have several legitimate lengths. Ask for the relevant front or rear measurement rather than comparing whichever number is easiest to read with a chart that uses another endpoint.

03

Read sleeves, shoulders and cuffs together

A shoulder seam can sit at the natural shoulder, drop down the arm or be replaced by raglan construction. That changes what seam-to-cuff length means. Choose landmarks that exist on both the warehouse jacket and your reference. Include whether the cuff is counted and whether the sleeve follows a straight or curved path.

Look at both sleeves laid in a comparable position. One bent elbow or turned cuff can suggest a length difference that the garment does not have. If the measurement is important, request a controlled view with the tape start and final mark visible. Do not estimate a missing length from screen pixels or the apparent size of the background grid.

Inspect cuff attachments, tabs and adjustment hardware where present. A closed cuff photograph cannot establish the full adjustment range. If the opening matters for your use, ask for the specific available measurement or view rather than assuming that a visible tab guarantees enough room.

04

Check front alignment with the closure arranged correctly

For a zip jacket, review a front view with the zipper closed naturally when available. Compare the center line, hem levels, collar halves and pocket positions. For buttons or snaps, confirm that the front is fastened as intended. An unfastened panel hanging differently can produce a false impression of unequal length or rotated construction.

Look for visibly missing teeth, a damaged pull, an open attachment seam or fabric caught beside the zipper. Protective flaps may cover parts of the closure intentionally, so request a located view if a critical area is obscured. Match the visible closure design to the ordered version instead of assuming every jacket uses the same hardware.

A photograph of a closed zipper is not proof that it runs smoothly. If operation matters, ask whether an appropriate handling check is available and what it involves. Do not infer waterproof sealing, corrosion resistance or future reliability from the color, finish or apparent thickness of the hardware.

05

Inspect collar, hood and pocket construction

Compare collar points or the shape of a stand collar using a centered image. A collar folded under on one side may appear shorter. Check where the collar or hood attaches to the neckline, looking for obvious gaps, trapped material or an attachment that seems twisted across multiple views. If the hood is removable, count its visible fasteners against the design.

For pockets, compare the intended number, position and closure type. A welt pocket may appear closed because of temporary basting, while a decorative flap may not indicate a full compartment. Use the listing and available service clarification before declaring a pocket unusable. Do not ask for cutting or removal of stitching without confirming what it is and the consequences.

A focused request can read: ‘Please photograph the left front pocket opening and its upper seam with the flap lifted, keeping the surrounding front panel visible.’ That supplies both the detail and its location. An isolated close-up can be difficult to connect to the correct pocket or side.

06

Review lining and padding with realistic limits

Ask for the jacket opened so the lining, inner pockets and visible seams can be reviewed. Check for an obvious tear, a loose attachment, uneven hanging or missing features specified for the ordered option. Lining often has extra ease to allow movement; a fold alone does not establish poor construction. Compare the suspect area with the garment arranged naturally.

For quilted or padded styles, inspect visible stitching channels and major irregularities in appearance. Compression from packing can alter loft in photos, and an exterior image cannot measure fill amount or prove insulation performance. Do not diagnose a missing internal layer solely from a flat section under uneven light.

Care and composition labels provide written claims, not independent material verification. Photos cannot establish warmth ratings, breathability, waterproof performance or long-term resistance to seam failure. If your intended use depends on those properties, you need evidence appropriate to them. A warehouse visual checklist should not be promoted into a technical certification.

07

Resolve a disagreement without inventing a tolerance

Hypothetical example: the seller chart lists a sleeve length of 64 cm, while a warehouse image seems to show 61 cm. The chart might use neck-to-cuff and the photo shoulder-to-cuff, or one may exclude the cuff. Establish the method before calling the difference a sizing defect. These invented values illustrate the reasoning and are not product specifications.

If the methods match and a clear repeat still lies outside the range you chose from your reference jacket, preserve that evidence. Your acceptable range should reflect the garment and your priorities, not a universal tolerance copied from another buyer. Do not select the most favorable of several inconsistent readings and ignore the rest.

Record the order size, label, endpoints, units, readable result and any uncertainty in one short note. Then decide whether one additional image would resolve the problem. If the remaining question is comfort while moving or warmth outdoors, more still photographs are unlikely to supply the missing evidence.

08

Complete the review before packing choices

Proceed when identity, important measurements and visible construction meet your preset requirements and the remaining unknowns are acceptable. Hold for clarification when the lining, measurement endpoint or closure detail is missing. Use the live after-sales options for a supported mismatch while eligible, keeping the order record and image references together.

Check current service availability, photo charges and deadlines before requesting anything; this article does not promise a particular turnaround or return outcome. Resolve QC before discussing compression or packaging removal. Packing changes may affect the shape and what can still be photographed. The final record should show why you chose to ship, clarify or seek a correction, based on evidence the gallery actually contains.
