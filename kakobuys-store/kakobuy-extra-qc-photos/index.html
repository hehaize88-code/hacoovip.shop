Kakobuy Extra QC Photos: Requests That Resolve a Real Doubt

Request useful Kakobuy extra QC photos with clear templates for measurements, labels, marks and missing parts. Know what to check before shipping.

The best reason to request extra Kakobuy QC photos is a question that could change your shipping decision. A hidden size label, an unreadable tape endpoint or a possible cut needs a different image from a general front view. This guide turns those uncertainties into specific requests. The examples are independent editorial templates, not official service instructions or reports of completed customer orders.

At a glance

Identify the warehouse item, describe the unresolved detail, specify the arrangement and camera angle, and explain the evidence needed. Check the available service, current charge and order deadline before submitting. Compare the reply with the original question before approving the parcel.

01

Identify the question before buying more images

Open the latest gallery and the exact order line together. Confirm that the item, color and selected size match before asking about a detail. Record which existing image raised the concern. If several similar items arrived, include the order or warehouse reference available in your account so that the request cannot easily be attached to the wrong piece.

Write one sentence describing the decision: you need to know whether the chest width suits your reference hoodie, whether a line is surface damage, or whether the promised detachable strap is present. If either answer would leave your decision unchanged, another photo may add little value. Prioritize a wrong variant, a critical dimension or a visible condition problem over cosmetic curiosity.

Separate missing evidence from negative evidence. A photograph that does not show the inside pocket does not establish that the pocket is missing. An image that clearly shows an empty attachment point may justify a more focused request. Keep those observations distinct when contacting support; a factual description is easier to investigate than an assumed diagnosis.

02

Use a four-part request

A practical request contains identity, arrangement, view and purpose. Identity selects the warehouse item. Arrangement explains how to place or open it. View specifies the camera position or detail. Purpose makes the question explicit. Keep each requested image readable on its own; a long paragraph containing six different measurements can be harder to execute accurately.

Template: ‘For this warehouse item, please lay the front flat without stretching it. Photograph straight across the underarm seams with a centimeter tape. Please keep the zero mark and both seam endpoints visible. I need to compare the flat chest width with a garment I already own.’ Replace the feature and endpoints rather than copying a template that does not fit the product.

Use ordinary landmarks: lower left front corner, right shoe outer midsole, inside back-neck label, or base of the left handle. Avoid directions such as ‘over there’ or unexplained abbreviations. If image annotation is supported, preserve the original photo and mark a separate copy. A tightly cropped screenshot can otherwise remove the context needed to identify the area.

03

Request a measurement that can be compared

Name both endpoints and the unit. For clothing, state whether the garment should be relaxed, flat or stretched; relaxed and stretched waistband measurements answer different questions. For sleeve length, distinguish shoulder seam to cuff from neck point to cuff. For a bag, distinguish base width from width at the opening. A number without a method is difficult to use.

Ask for the tape to lie in the same plane as the feature and for both endpoints to remain visible. The camera should face the measurement as directly as practical. A ruler in the background is not a reliable scale for an item closer to the lens. If the first visible tape mark is not zero, do not silently read the final mark as the whole length.

Measure your reference item by the same method before comparing. A flat garment width is not a body circumference, and outsole length is not usable shoe space. Decide your acceptable range in advance. A photo provides limited measurement evidence; it cannot promise comfort, stretch recovery, drape or a perfect fit.

04

Use two views for a suspected mark

For a possible scratch, stain or open seam, request a wider locating view and a close view. The wider image connects the detail to the correct item. The close image should show the boundary of the mark and nearby intact material. A close-up alone can be sharp yet impossible to place within the product.

Template: ‘Please photograph the line beside the lower right front seam in even light, with one wider view showing its location and one closer view. Please also show the same area from a slightly different angle so I can distinguish a reflection from a surface mark.’ This asks for observable evidence without claiming that the mark is already a defect.

Compare whether the feature stays in the same physical location across views. Reflections can move with the camera or light, while a real mark can still change visibility at different angles. Neither disappearance nor persistence alone proves the cause. If photographs remain inconclusive, record the uncertainty instead of demanding a durability or material guarantee that a gallery cannot provide.

05

Check labels, contents and hidden areas

For a label, request a readable image with enough surrounding item visible to connect the label to that piece. Include the sizing system when relevant. A box label and a shoe label are separate evidence; one does not establish what the other says. Photographing the wrong label more clearly will not resolve a variant mismatch.

Template: ‘Please arrange the item and the accessories from this order together in one photo, including the detachable strap and any separate hardware. Please show the inside compartment with the opening unobstructed.’ Adapt the list to what the order actually includes. Do not add accessories that were never promised in the listing.

Some areas may require unsealing packaging or handling a removable component. Check whether that is available and whether it affects return conditions before authorizing it. A visual check of a zipper can show its position, but a still photograph does not demonstrate smooth operation. Ask about available handling checks separately instead of treating a photo as a functional test.

06

Check timing and cost in the live order

This guide does not assign a universal price, included photo count, response time or button name. Those details need confirmation in the current account and service terms. Before requesting anything, check what is offered for the item, the quoted charge if any, and the return or exchange deadline applicable to that order.

Do not assume that sending a question pauses another deadline. Record the displayed deadline and use the available support or after-sales route promptly if waiting for a reply could remove an option. Warehouse storage time and seller return eligibility are different limits. An item remaining in storage does not by itself extend the period for disputing a mismatch.

Keep the request proportionate to the decision. One well-defined image may resolve a size concern more effectively than a large set of new angles. Conversely, paying for another photo makes little sense if it cannot establish the property you need, such as long-term wear, waterproof performance or fiber composition. Those questions require different evidence.

07

Review the reply against the original uncertainty

Do not accept a reply simply because new images arrived. Match them to the correct item, check the requested arrangement, and confirm that the actual question is answered. A new front photo still fails a request for a readable neck label. A chest tape hidden under a folded sleeve still fails an endpoint check.

Worked example, using invented numbers: your reference sweatshirt measures 58 cm flat across the chest, and you have chosen 57–59 cm as your personal range. The first warehouse image ends near 58 but hides the starting point. That image is unresolved. A repeat showing zero at one seam and 58 at the other, with the garment relaxed, supports the comparison. The range is an illustration, not a sizing recommendation.

Save the original gallery, your request and the reply together. Record the result as resolved, unresolved or outside your chosen range. If the repeat still misses the requirement, explain the missing endpoint or view precisely. Repeating ‘more photos please’ makes another unhelpful response more likely.

08

Finish with an explicit parcel decision

Proceed when the ordered identity is correct, the important visible checks pass and the remaining unknowns are acceptable to you. Hold the item when a specific unanswered question can change the outcome. If clear evidence shows a material mismatch, use the live after-sales options while the order remains eligible and preserve the supporting images.

A complete record needs only the order fact, the observed fact, the image location and the next step. For example: ‘Ordered size M; attached label reads L; please check the order variant and advise the available correction.’ This is more actionable than a broad complaint about quality. Useful additional photos make a decision clearer; their value is the uncertainty removed, not the number of files received.
